Xuanzang

Person
Recorded range · 606 CE — 1592 CE9 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 玄奘 / 唐僧

Xuanzang (602–664) was born in Gou in Luo prefecture and became a monk as a youth, studying Buddhist texts intensively. Dissatisfied with ambiguities and gaps in Chinese translations, he left Chang'an in 629, traveled through the Hexi corridor and the Western Regions into India, studied Yogacara at Nalanda, toured Indian regions and participated in debates. He returned to Chang'an in 645 with a large collection of Sanskrit texts and images and was received by Emperor Taizong. He then directed translations of the Great Perfection of Wisdom Sutra and the Yogacarabhumi and dictated the Great Tang Records on the Western Regions, documenting the geography, politics, religion and customs of Central Asia and India. He died in 664. Xuanzang's travel and translation brought Indian Buddhist knowledge systematically into China and left indispensable primary material for later study of Central and South Asian history. His translation team used strict division of labor and Sanskrit-Chinese comparison and adopted principles such as “five kinds of non-translation.” The Great Tang Records on the Western Regions remains an important reference for archaeology and history in Agni, Kucha and the Indian states.
