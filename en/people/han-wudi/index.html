Han Wudi

Person
Recorded range · 156 BCE — 118 BCE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 汉武帝 / 刘彻

Emperor Wu of Han (156–87 BCE), personal name Liu Che, was the son of Emperor Jing and came to the throne in 141 BCE. He promoted Confucianism as state doctrine, founded the Imperial Academy and set a direction for selecting officials through classical learning; he also established state monopolies on salt and iron, the equable transportation and price stabilization systems and property taxes, concentrating revenue at the center. Militarily he sent Wei Qing and Huo Qubing against the Xiongnu, seized the Hexi Corridor and sent Zhang Qian to the Western Regions, opening contacts between the Central Plains and Central Asia. In his later years constant warfare, the witchcraft persecutions and heavy taxes exhausted society, and he issued the Luntai edict to adjust policy. He died in 87 BCE. Emperor Wu turned Han from Huang-Lao quietism toward active expansion and institutional integration, at the cost of population loss, financial strain and harsh officials. His expansion brought Han territory and influence to a peak but emptied the treasury and eroded the tax base, forcing a more conservative turn in his last years. He established regional inspectors to supervise local government and strengthened central control over the commanderies and kingdoms. His reign also left lasting institutional legacies in the era-name system, the Taichu calendar and the expansion of the Music Bureau.
