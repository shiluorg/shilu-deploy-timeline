Zhang Juzheng

Person
Recorded range · 1454 CE — 1588 CE9 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 张居正 / 张太岳

Zhang Juzheng (1525–1582), courtesy name Shuda, was a native of Jiangling and passed the jinshi in 1547. He entered the Grand Secretariat in the late Longqing reign and became chief grand secretary in the early Wanli reign, directing government while the Wanli emperor was young and Empress Dowager Li cooperated with the eunuch Feng Bao. He introduced the kao-cheng method of deadlines and performance review to discipline the bureaucracy, surveyed land to broaden the tax base, promoted the single-whip method that merged corvée and tax into silver payments, and strengthened frontier defense with generals such as Qi Jiguang. The reforms improved fiscal and administrative efficiency in the short term but provoked fierce opposition through centralization, the “grabbing love” affair and suppression of censors. After his death in 1582 the Wanli emperor purged his family and most reforms were undone. Zhang's strength was execution; his weakness was making institutions depend on personal authority. His reforms depended on personal authority and an alliance with the eunuch Feng Bao rather than institutional support. After his death his household was confiscated, his eldest son committed suicide, his family was exiled and most of his reforms were reversed. His reforms supported finances and frontier defense in the early Wanli reign and showed that bureaucratic performance review could work in the short term.
