Isaac Newton

Person
Recorded range · 1643 CE — 1738 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 牛顿 / 艾萨克·牛顿

Isaac Newton (1643–1727) was born at Woolsthorpe, England, and entered Cambridge in 1661. During the plague years of 1665–1666 he returned home and worked on calculus, optics and gravitation, developing the fluxional method and experiments on color dispersion. In 1687 he published the Principia Mathematica, unifying terrestrial and celestial motion through three laws of motion and universal gravitation and establishing the mathematical framework of classical mechanics. In 1704 the Opticks summarized refraction, dispersion and the reflecting telescope. In later life Newton served as Warden and Master of the Royal Mint and President of the Royal Society and became involved in disputes over the priority of calculus. He died in 1727. Newton did not discover gravity through an apple; he integrated Kepler, Galileo and his own mathematics into a calculable system, and his theological and alchemical studies were equally serious. His universal gravitation made celestial prediction possible and provided a model for the Enlightenment view of natural law. He left a large body of manuscripts, and only later generations gradually organized his mathematical and theological notes.
