Abraham Lincoln

Person
Recorded range · 910 CE — 1865 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 林肯 / 亚伯拉罕·林肯

Abraham Lincoln (1809–1865) was born on the Kentucky frontier and grew up in Indiana and Illinois, acquiring an education largely through self-study and manual work before becoming a lawyer. He entered Whig politics in the 1840s and gained national attention by opposing the extension of slavery and the Mexican-American War; in his 1858 debates with Stephen Douglas he argued that a house divided against itself could not stand. After his election in 1860, southern states seceded and the Civil War began. Lincoln made preservation of the Union his first objective, but the Emancipation Proclamation, the Gettysburg Address and his push for the Thirteenth Amendment turned the war into a struggle against slavery. He was assassinated at Ford's Theatre in 1865, before Reconstruction could be carried through. His significance lies less in winning the war than in placing the indivisibility of the Union and the abolition of slavery at the center of American constitutional language. He refused to recognize the legality of southern secession and brought conscription, finance and railroads under federal power, expanding the capacity of the modern state.
