Sima Yi

Person
Recorded range · 227 CE — 251 CE10 event anchors1 storiesAlso recorded as 司马懿 / 仲达

Sima Yi (179–251), courtesy name Zhongda, was a native of Wen in Henei and came from a gentry family. He entered office under Cao Cao and served the Wei emperors Wen and Ming, long directing war against Shu Han and facing Zhuge Liang at Qishan and Wuzhang Plains. He favored defense and logistics over decisive battle, wearing opponents down with time. After Emperor Ming's death in 239 he shared the regency with Cao Shuang; in 249 he launched the Gaopingling coup, killed Cao Shuang and took control of Wei's military and government. He died in 251. Sima Yi did not take the throne, but his sons Sima Shi and Sima Zhao continued to weaken Wei imperial power, and his grandson Sima Yan eventually replaced Wei with Jin. Later tradition, especially Romance of the Three Kingdoms, made him a suspicious and patient schemer; historically he was closer to a hereditary official who built military households, marriage ties and local networks over decades. Through military merit, marriage and recommendation he embedded the Sima family in Wei's power networks, providing the organizational base for Jin's replacement of Wei. After the Gaopingling coup he did not immediately take the throne but eliminated rivals in the name of regency.
