Qianlong

Person
Recorded range · 1735 CE — 1790 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 乾隆 / 弘历 / 清高宗

The Qianlong Emperor (1711–1799), personal name Hongli, was the fourth son of Yongzheng and came to the throne in 1735. During his reign he suppressed the Dzungars and the Muslim rebellions in Xinjiang and incorporated the region into the empire; he commissioned the Siku Quanshu, built the Yuanmingyuan and the Chengde mountain resort, toured the south repeatedly and reformed salt administration and river works. He was diligent early in his reign but later favored Heshen, allowing corruption and fiscal strain to grow, and the White Lotus rebellion broke out around his abdication. He abdicated to Jiaqing in 1796 and died in 1799. Qianlong's “Ten Great Campaigns” expanded Qing territory but imposed heavy military costs and frontier burdens; the Siku Quanshu preserved texts while accompanying book bans and literary inquisitions. His reign encapsulates the Qing transition from peak to decline. The Siku Quanshu compiled under his patronage included more than three thousand works, but bans and deletions also caused documentary losses. In old age he called himself the “Ten Complete Old Man,” but frontier wars, southern tours and Heshen's corruption cracked the narrative of prosperity. His court collections later became an important source of holdings in the Palace Museum and the National Palace Museum in Taipei.
