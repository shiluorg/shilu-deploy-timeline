Sun Quan

Person
Recorded range · 200 CE — 230 CE10 event anchors5 storiesAlso recorded as 孙权 / 仲谋 / Sun Zhongmou

Sun Quan (182–252), courtesy name Zhongmou, was a native of Fuchun in Wu commandery and the second son of Sun Jian and younger brother of Sun Ce. After Sun Ce's assassination in 200 he took over the Jiangdong base, stabilizing it through officials and generals such as Zhang Zhao, Zhou Yu and Lu Su. In 208 he allied with Liu Bei and defeated Cao Cao at Red Cliffs; he later seized Jing province, killed Guan Yu in 219 and defeated Liu Bei at Yiling in 222. In 229 he declared himself emperor, founded Wu and fixed the capital at Jianye. In old age he became suspicious, and the rivalry between the heir Sun He and the Prince of Lu, Sun Ba, produced the “Two Palaces” affair, splitting the court and weakening the state. He died in 252. Sun Quan was skilled at balancing Wei and Shu and depended on Jiangdong magnates and the Yangtze fleet; his rule allowed a southern regime to confront the north over the long term for the first time. In old age he deposed the heir and killed ministers, showing that the Jiangdong regime never solved succession and gentry balance. Wu relied on the Yangtze defense and shipbuilding and connected to Southeast Asia through maritime trade. After his death Wu fell into struggles between powerful ministers and the royal clan and was conquered by Western Jin in 280.
