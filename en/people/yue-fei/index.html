Yue Fei

Person
Recorded range · 1129 CE — 1141 CE7 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 岳飞 / 岳鹏举

Yue Fei (1103–1142) was born in Tangyin, Xiangzhou, to a farming family. He joined the army at the end of the Northern Song and became a leading anti-Jin commander in the early Southern Song; his troops were known for discipline as the “Yue family army.” He defeated Jin and puppet Qi forces in Xiang-Han, Yancheng and Yingchang, advocated a northern campaign to recover the Central Plains and opposed peace with Jin. In 1141 Emperor Gaozong and Qin Hui recalled Yue Fei and stripped him of command; in 1142 he was executed on the pretext of “unnecessary” charges. After his death the Southern Song court gradually rehabilitated him and honored him as Prince of E. Later tradition made him a loyal minister and national hero, but he was also entangled in the early Southern Song politics of military power and succession anxiety; his tragedy shows that battlefield victory does not automatically translate into political safety. Whether the poem “Man Jiang Hong” is by Yue Fei remains disputed, but the work and his image together shaped later memories of loyalty. The Southern Song later honored him as Prince of E and built a temple in Hangzhou. His military talent and political tragedy together formed the sharpest memory of the early Southern Song.
