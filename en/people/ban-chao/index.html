Ban Chao

Person
Recorded range · 72 CE — 87 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 班超 / 定远侯

Ban Chao (32–102), courtesy name Zhongsheng, was a native of Anling in Fufeng and the son of Ban Biao and younger brother of Ban Gu. Poor in his early years, he supported his family by copying texts before turning to military service. In 73 he joined Dou Gu's campaign against the Northern Xiongnu and was soon sent to the Western Regions as a acting commander. At Shanshan he discovered the arrival of a Xiongnu mission and attacked its camp with thirty-six men, forcing the Shanshan king to submit; he then operated in Khotan and Kashgar and built local alliances against the Xiongnu and Kucha. In 91 he became Protector General of the Western Regions, stationed at Qiuci, and controlled both the northern and southern routes. He returned to Luoyang in 102 and died that year. Ban Chao sustained Han influence with small forces, diplomatic intimidation and local alliances, but his success depended on personal judgment and court supply and was difficult for successors to reproduce. After his death the Western Regions remained unstable, and the Eastern Han withdrew its protector general in 107, showing how difficult it was to sustain a frontier order built by one man without steady court financing. He relied on the troops and supply routes of oasis states such as Shanshan, Khotan and Kashgar rather than on long-term provisioning from the Central Plains.
