Cao Pi

Person
Recorded range · 217 CE — 226 CE10 event anchors4 storiesAlso recorded as 曹丕 / 子桓

Cao Pi (187–226), courtesy name Zihuan, was a native of Qiao in Pei and the second son of Cao Cao. He accompanied the army as a youth and later spent long periods at Ye, where he participated in Jian'an literary circles. After Cao Cao's death in 220 he inherited the offices of King of Wei and Chancellor, and in the same year accepted Emperor Xian's abdication, founded Wei, fixed the capital at Luoyang and adopted the Huangchu era. On Chen Qun's advice he introduced the nine-rank system, which partly institutionalized recruitment through impartial judges and family standing; it stabilized cooperation with the elite in the short term but strengthened oligarchy over the long term. He campaigned repeatedly against Wu and Shu without completing unification, while reforming administration, restoring the Imperial Academy and compiling the Wei code. He died in 226 and was succeeded by Cao Rui. Cao Pi's decisive contribution was not another great campaign but the conversion of Cao Cao's military household into imperial and bureaucratic government. The nine-rank system was later captured by great families and became the institutional gateway to aristocratic politics in the Wei, Jin and Northern and Southern dynasties. He died at only forty, and Wei imperial power soon fell into the regency pattern dominated by the Sima family. His essay “On Literature” treated writing as a great enterprise of statecraft and became an important starting point for later Chinese literary criticism.
