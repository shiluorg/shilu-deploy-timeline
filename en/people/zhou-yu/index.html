Zhou Yu

Person
Recorded range · 200 CE — 210 CE5 event anchors3 storiesAlso recorded as 周瑜 / 公瑾

Zhou Yu (175–210), courtesy name Gongjin, was a native of Shu in Lujiang, from a gentry family and a friend of Sun Ce. After Sun Ce raised troops, Zhou Yu joined him and helped pacify Jiangdong; after Sun Ce's death in 200 he served Sun Quan as front commander. In 208, when Cao Cao moved south, Zhou Yu and Lu Su advocated alliance with Liu Bei and commanded the fleet that defeated Cao Cao at Red Cliffs by fire, establishing the three-way division. He later took Nan commandery and proposed a strategy to seize Yi province in the west and Xiangyang in the north, but died at Baqiu in 210 while preparing the Sichuan campaign. Zhou Yu was accomplished in music and known for his bearing; later literature made him the central figure of Red Cliffs, but historically he was one of Jiangdong's main military and diplomatic designers. Before Red Cliffs he analyzed Cao Cao's troops' unfamiliarity with the terrain and overextended supply lines, strengthening Sun Quan's resolve. His early death deprived Jiangdong of a strategic designer, and Lu Su and Lü Meng subsequently adjusted policy toward Jing province. His musical accomplishment and gentry bearing gave him influence among both civil and military elites in Jiangdong. After his death Sun Quan wore mourning and publicly grieved.
