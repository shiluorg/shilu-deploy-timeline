Suleiman

Person
Recorded range · 778 CE — 1566 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 苏莱曼 / 苏莱曼大帝

Suleiman (1494–1566) was born in Trabzon, son of Sultan Selim I, and came to the throne in 1520. He took Belgrade and Rhodes, defeated Hungary at Mohács in 1526, besieged Vienna unsuccessfully in 1529 and then contested the Mediterranean and Central Europe with the Habsburgs for decades. At home he reformed the legal code, taxation and judiciary and was known as “the Lawgiver”; he patronized the architect Sinan, who built the Süleymaniye Mosque and many public works, and promoted grand viziers such as Ibrahim. In his later years a son's rebellion and palace and succession struggles intensified, and he died on campaign in Hungary in 1566. Suleiman brought the Ottoman Empire to its height, but his rule depended on conquest revenue and personal authority and prepared later fiscal and succession crises. He contested Mesopotamia with the Safavid empire and pushed the eastern frontier to Baghdad. His rule also depended on palace elites trained through the devshirme system.
