Wang Anshi

Person
Recorded range · 1068 CE — 1086 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 王安石 / 王介甫

Wang Anshi (1021–1086), courtesy name Jiefu, was a native of Linchuan in Fuzhou and passed the jinshi in 1042. He served long in local office, learned finance, waterworks and livelihood and argued for “managing wealth” and reform. From 1069, with Emperor Shenzong's support, he introduced the New Policies: green sprouts loans, corvée exemption, market exchange, village mutual defense, land survey and equalized tax, seeking to solve fiscal and defense problems through state credit, markets and militia. The reforms were opposed by Sima Guang, Su Shi and the old faction, and implementation produced arbitrary levies, local disruption and administrative abuse; he was twice removed as chancellor and died in 1086. Wang Anshi was both a writer and an experimenter in institutions; his failure does not prove reform was unnecessary but shows how difficult top-level design was to implement where bureaucratic capacity was limited and interest groups strong. His green sprouts law was meant to replace usury but became compulsory levying in practice; the corvée exemption also redistributed burdens at the grassroots. His prose and poetry are concise and forceful, and he is counted among the Eight Great Prose Masters of the Tang and Song.
