Zhang Qian

Person
Recorded range · 142 BCE — 115 BCE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 张骞 / 博望侯

Zhang Qian (c. 164–114 BCE) was born in Chenggu, Hanzhong, and served as a palace gentleman under Emperor Wu of Han. In 138 BCE he was sent to the Yuezhi to propose a joint attack on the Xiongnu; he was detained by the Xiongnu for more than ten years, escaped and reached the Yuezhi through Dayuan and Kangju, only to find that they no longer wished to return east. He returned to Chang'an in 126 BCE with information on the geography, products and politics of Central Asian states. In 119 BCE he went again to the Wusun and sent deputy envoys to Dayuan, Kangju, Daxia, Anxi and other states, promoting direct Han contact with the Western Regions. Zhang Qian's journeys did not create a military alliance, but they opened the official Silk Road and gave the Han court its first systematic knowledge of the world beyond the Pamirs. He died in 114 BCE. The grapes, alfalfa and information about fine horses that he brought back changed Han agriculture and military horizons. Both missions involved military failure and diplomatic frustration, yet his information and routes had a long-term impact. His deputy envoys reached Anxi, and from then on the Han court knew of a vast trading network east of Rome.
