Li Shimin

Person
Recorded range · 598 CE — 626 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 李世民 / 唐太宗

Li Shimin (598–649), Emperor Taizong of Tang, was born at Wugong, the second son of Li Yuan, founder of the Tang. In the late Sui he joined his father's uprising, commanded troops that pacified the Guanzhong and Central Plains and defeated Dou Jiande and Wang Shichong at Hulao, laying the basis for Tang unification. In 626 he carried out the Xuanwu Gate coup, killing his brothers Li Jiancheng and Li Yuanji, and then took the throne under the Zhenguan era. He refined the Three Departments and Six Ministries, revised the Zhenguan code, promoted equal-field land allocation and the zu-yong-diao tax system, and stressed clean administration and remonstrance, producing the narrative of the Zhenguan governance. Abroad he defeated the Eastern Turks and operated in the Western Regions, and northern peoples honored him as Heavenly Qaghan; he also welcomed Xuanzang back from India and supported translation of Buddhist texts. He died in 649 and was succeeded by Gaozong. Later generations idealized his rule, but the Xuanwu Gate coup and the violence of imperial succession remained part of its legitimacy. His Di Fan summarized statecraft for the crown prince and reflects his anxiety about succession. After his death Wu Zetian gradually took power, showing that Zhenguan politics had not solved the structural problem of palace and royal-family interference.
