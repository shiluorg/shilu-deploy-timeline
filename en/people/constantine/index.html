Constantine

Person
Recorded range · 272 CE — 321 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 君士坦丁 / 君士坦丁大帝

Constantine (c. 272–337) was born at Naissus, the son of a Western Roman military officer. In 312 he defeated Maxentius at the Milvian Bridge, and in 313 he and Licinius issued the Edict of Milan, ending persecution of Christians; in 324 he defeated Licinius and became sole ruler. In 330 he moved the capital to Constantinople on the Bosphorus, creating a new city linking the Balkans and Anatolia. In 325 he convened the Council of Nicaea and intervened in Christian doctrinal disputes, though he was baptized only on his deathbed. His reforms reorganized coinage, bureaucracy and the army, and made the relationship between emperor and church a central problem of imperial politics. He may not have been a sincere convert, but he prepared the way for Christianity to become an imperial religion. Whether he was sincerely converted or used Christianity as a unifying instrument remains disputed. He built churches and endowed the church while retaining traditional sacrifices; religious policy always served imperial order.
