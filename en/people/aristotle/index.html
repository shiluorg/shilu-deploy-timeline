Aristotle

Person
Recorded range · 384 BCE — 1260 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 亚里士多德

Aristotle (384–322 BCE) was born in Stagira in Macedonia and entered Plato's Academy as a young man, studying and later teaching there for about two decades. After Plato's death he traveled in Asia Minor and Macedonia and served as tutor to Alexander the Great. He returned to Athens in 335 BCE and founded the Lyceum, whose followers were called Peripatetics because of his habit of walking while lecturing. His work covered logic, physics, astronomy, biology, ethics, politics, rhetoric and poetry, and advanced the four causes, potentiality and actuality, teleology and the claim that the human being is a political animal. Much of his natural philosophy was later corrected, but his categories, syllogistic logic and division of disciplines shaped European and Islamic knowledge for centuries. He left Athens in 322 BCE and died the following year. Medieval Arabic philosophers and Thomas Aquinas called him “the Philosopher,” and his works became a mainstay of university education in the liberal arts. His school also collected manuscripts and biological specimens, encouraging empirical research.
