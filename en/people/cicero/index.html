Cicero

Person
Recorded range · 106 BCE — 1344 CE9 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 西塞罗 / Marcus Tullius Cicero

Cicero (106–43 BCE) was born into an equestrian family at Arpinum and studied law, rhetoric and philosophy in Rome as a young man. As consul in 63 BCE he suppressed the Catilinarian conspiracy and left four speeches that became models of political rhetoric. In 58 BCE he was exiled for executing the conspirators without trial and recalled the following year. During the late Republic he tried to preserve constitutional order among Caesar, Pompey and the Senate; after Caesar's assassination he attacked Antony in the Philippics and was proscribed and killed by the Second Triumvirate in 43 BCE. His speeches, letters and philosophical dialogues preserved the political experience of the late Republic and shaped later Latin education and republican language. The republic he defended was not equal, but his formulations of checks on power and the rule of law had lasting influence. His letters reveal the inner workings of late republican decision-making and have been used repeatedly since the Renaissance as models for political education and Latin prose. He was both a politician and a philosopher, trying to use Greek philosophy to provide behavioral standards for the Roman elite.
