Liu Bei

Person
Recorded range · 194 CE — 217 CE10 event anchors4 storiesAlso recorded as 刘备 / 玄德 / Liu Xuande

Liu Bei (161–223), courtesy name Xuande, was a native of Zhuo commandery who claimed descent from Emperor Jing. At the end of the Eastern Han he raised troops and attached himself successively to Gongsun Zan, Tao Qian, Cao Cao, Yuan Shao and Liu Biao without securing a stable base. In 207 he visited Zhuge Liang three times and adopted a strategy of allying with Sun Quan against Cao Cao and holding Jing and Yi provinces; in 208 he joined Sun Quan to defeat Cao Cao at Red Cliffs, later took part of Jing and entered Yi in 214 and became King of Hanzhong in 219. After Cao Pi replaced Han in 221, Liu Bei declared himself emperor in Chengdu under the dynastic name Han; in 222 he campaigned against Sun Quan over Guan Yu's death, was crushingly defeated at Yiling and died at Baidicheng in 223. Liu Bei drew support through his claim to Han descent and an image of benevolence, but Shu Han had the weakest base, and the loss of Jing and the defeat at Yiling made the Longzhong plan impossible to complete. Shu Han survived for more than forty years from the single base of Yi province through the successive regencies of Zhuge Liang, Jiang Wan and Fei Yi. On his deathbed he entrusted the state to Zhuge Liang, creating the narrative of mutual trust between ruler and minister while exposing the fragility of Shu Han's succession.
