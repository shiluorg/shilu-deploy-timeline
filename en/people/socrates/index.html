Socrates

Person
Recorded range · 470 BCE — 380 BCE8 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 苏格拉底

Socrates (470–399 BCE) was born in Athens, the son of a stonemason and a midwife. He left no writings and is known mainly through Plato and Xenophon. He debated in the marketplace, gymnasium and with young men, pressing questions about definitions, exposing contradictions and insisting on “know thyself” and virtue as knowledge. He criticized the lot and demagogy of Athenian democracy and was accused in 399 BCE of rejecting the city's gods and corrupting the young; he was condemned and drank hemlock. Socrates built no system, but he turned philosophy from natural inquiry toward ethics and public life, and his death became a symbol of the conflict between freedom of thought and civic authority. He refused to escape, arguing that obeying the city's laws was a citizen's duty, a choice that made the trial more symbolic. His method was called “midwifery”: helping interlocutors discover through questioning that they did not really know. His trial and death were recorded by Plato in the Apology and Phaedo and became a starting point for Western philosophy's self-understanding.
