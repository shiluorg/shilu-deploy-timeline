Zheng He

Person
Recorded range · 1371 CE — 1433 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 郑和 / 三宝太监

Zheng He (1371–1433) was born in Kunyang, Yunnan, a Hui Muslim, and early entered the household of the Prince of Yan, Zhu Di, serving with merit in the Jingnan campaign and receiving the surname Zheng. From 1405 to 1433 he led seven voyages through Southeast Asia, the Indian Ocean, the Arabian Sea and the East African coast, reaching as far as Malindi and Sofala. The fleet included treasure ships, supply ships and warships and carried silk, porcelain and copper cash, maintaining Ming maritime order through tribute, trade and force. Zheng He's voyages were unprecedented in scale but established no colonies or permanent navy; accompanying officials recorded local products, religions and politics. He died at sea on the return voyage in 1433. After him the Ming turned toward maritime prohibition and long-distance voyages ended; his legacy became an important symbol in later debates about Chinese sea power and openness. His fleets relied mainly on peaceful tribute and used force only in places such as Ceylon and Samudera. After the Ming ended the voyages, private trade along the southeast coast grew, and maritime prohibition coexisted with smuggling.
