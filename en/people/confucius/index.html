Confucius

Person
Recorded range · 600 BCE — 481 BCE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 孔子 / 孔丘 / 仲尼

Confucius (551–479 BCE) was born in Zou in the state of Lu. His family had declined, and he worked in minor offices managing granaries and accounts before gathering disciples and teaching. He advocated benevolence, ritual propriety and government by virtue, insisting that rulers set an example rather than rely on punishment alone. In middle age he traveled among the states seeking a ruler who would put his political ideals into practice, but he was never given lasting office. In old age he returned to Lu, arranged classics such as the Odes and Documents and is traditionally credited with editing the Spring and Autumn Annals. He left no systematic writings; his sayings were recorded by disciples in the Analects. His teaching challenged aristocratic monopoly by accepting students regardless of background, and his thought, developed by Mencius, Xunzi and later classical scholars, became a shared resource for politics, ethics and education in East Asia. After his death his disciples divided into different schools, and from the Han dynasty onward he was absorbed into state sacrifice and the examination system, his image changing with each era. The Analects present his judgments in short sayings and dialogues; they must be read against ritual institutions and the politics of his time, not treated as a modern management manual.
