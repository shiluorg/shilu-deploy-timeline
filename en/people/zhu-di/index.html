Zhu Di

Person
Recorded range · 1398 CE — 1424 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 朱棣 / 永乐帝 / 明成祖

Zhu Di (1360–1424) was born in Yingtian, the fourth son of Zhu Yuanzhang, and was enfeoffed as Prince of Yan to guard Beiping. In 1399 he launched the Jingnan campaign under the slogan of “clearing the ruler's side,” fought for three years, entered Nanjing in 1402 and took the throne from the Jianwen emperor under the Yongle era. He established the Grand Secretariat, commissioned the Yongle Encyclopedia, moved the capital to Beijing, built the Forbidden City, sent Zheng He on ocean voyages and campaigned in Annam and Mongolia. The northern capital strengthened Ming control over the frontier but increased grain transport and fiscal pressure. He died in 1424 on the return from a northern campaign. Zhu Di's legitimacy was always shadowed by the Jingnan coup; he used coercion and grand projects to create the Yongle prosperity, while leaving risks of eunuch interference and fiscal exhaustion. He established the Eastern Depot and relied on eunuchs, strengthening intelligence and palace control and setting a precedent for eunuch interference in Ming politics. His five northern campaigns against the Mongols struck the Tatars and Oirats but did not eliminate the steppe threat. The Yongle Encyclopedia compiled under his patronage preserved many lost works and became an important source for later reconstruction of texts.
