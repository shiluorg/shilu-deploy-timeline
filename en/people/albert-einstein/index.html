Albert Einstein

Person
Recorded range · 1879 CE — 1905 CE4 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 爱因斯坦 / 阿尔伯特·爱因斯坦

Albert Einstein (1879–1955) was born in Ulm, Germany, grew up in Munich and later studied in Italy and Switzerland, graduating from the Swiss Federal Institute of Technology in Zurich in 1900. Unable to obtain an academic post, he worked as an examiner at the Bern patent office. In 1905 he published a series of papers on the photoelectric effect, Brownian motion, special relativity and mass-energy equivalence that transformed the understanding of time, space, light and energy; in 1915 he completed general relativity, interpreting gravity as the curvature of spacetime. He received the 1921 Nobel Prize in Physics for the photoelectric effect rather than for relativity. After the Nazi seizure of power in 1933 he moved to the United States, worked at the Institute for Advanced Study in Princeton and publicly warned about the dangers of nuclear weapons. He spent his later years pursuing a unified field theory without completing it. His work became a foundation of modern physics and also made the public responsibility of scientists a modern question. Relativity was later tested repeatedly through eclipse observations, GPS and particle experiments, and became a working framework for engineering and cosmology.
