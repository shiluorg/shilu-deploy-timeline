George Washington

Person
Recorded range · 1732 CE — 1865 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 华盛顿 / 乔治·华盛顿

George Washington (1732–1799) was born into a Virginia planter family, worked as a surveyor, served in the French and Indian War and learned frontier warfare and land speculation. In 1775 the Continental Congress appointed him commander of the Continental Army; he held it together for eight years under conditions of scarce pay, weapons and training until the Treaty of Paris recognized American independence in 1783. He presided over the Constitutional Convention in 1787, became the first president in 1789, established cabinet, financial and diplomatic precedents and voluntarily retired in 1797. Washington owned enslaved people, a contradiction that ran through his political career, and he did not resolve the problems of Indigenous peoples and the frontier. His importance lies in placing the revolutionary army under civilian government and in establishing the precedent of voluntary transfer of power. He freed his enslaved people in his will, but the bequest took effect only after his wife's death, showing that abolition remained a private choice rather than public policy for him. He refused lifetime rule and left a precedent for later presidential term limits.
