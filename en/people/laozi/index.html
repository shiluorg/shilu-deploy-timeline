Laozi

Person
Recorded range · 585 BCE — 1014 CE8 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 老子 / 李耳

Laozi is traditionally said to have been a native of Ku in Chu in the late Spring and Autumn period and to have served as keeper of the archives of the Zhou court, managing texts and records. Confucius is said to have consulted him on ritual, but that account comes from later sources and cannot be treated as contemporary evidence. The received Daodejing, about five thousand characters, is divided into the Dao and De sections and speaks of “the Dao that follows nature,” “non-action in government” and “the soft overcoming the hard,” using short sayings, paradox and poetic images to warn against power, desire and excessive knowledge. Whether Laozi existed and when the book was composed remain disputed; the layered text from the Warring States to the early Han makes “Laozi” look more like a school tradition than a single author. His thought was repeatedly absorbed by Huang-Lao statecraft, Daoism, Dark Learning and later recluse culture and became an important resource for limiting intervention in Chinese political thought. Variants such as the Guodian bamboo slips and Mawangdui silk texts show that the text was continually rewritten in transmission. Daoism later honored him as Lord Lao and turned the philosophical text into a religious scripture. Its “non-action” is not passive idleness but an argument against excessive intervention and inflated titles.
