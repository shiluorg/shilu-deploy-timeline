Zhuge Liang

Person
Recorded range · 207 CE — 229 CE10 event anchors3 storiesAlso recorded as 诸葛亮 / 孔明 / Zhuge Kongming

Zhuge Liang (181–234), courtesy name Kongming, was a native of Yangdu in Langya; he moved to Jingzhou with his uncle during the wars and lived in retirement at Longzhong. In 207 Liu Bei visited him three times, and Zhuge Liang proposed allying with Sun Quan against Cao Cao and holding Jing and Yi provinces. After Red Cliffs in 208 he helped Liu Bei take parts of Jing, entered Yi in 214 and became chancellor after Liu Bei declared himself emperor in 221. After Liu Bei's death in 223 he served Liu Shan, directed civil and military affairs, pacified the south, repaired the alliance with Wu and launched repeated northern campaigns against Wei from 228, none of which achieved a decisive victory because of supply difficulties. He died at Wuzhang Plains in 234. Zhuge Liang's rule of law, military farming, transport inventions and “Memorial on the Expedition” created the image of tireless service, but Shu Han had the weakest base and his campaigns consumed its limited resources. He governed Shu with strict law and clean administration and paid attention to waterworks, salt and iron. His “Memorial on the Expedition” became a model of loyal-minister narrative, but the military goals of the northern campaigns never matched Shu's resources.
