Martin Luther

Person
Recorded range · 1483 CE — 1546 CE4 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 马丁·路德

Martin Luther (1483–1546) was born in Eisleben, Saxony, entered the Augustinian order in 1505 and later became professor of theology at Wittenberg. In 1517 he published the Ninety-Five Theses, attacking the sale of indulgences and insisting on faith and repentance rather than purchased remission. At the Diet of Worms in 1521 he refused to recant, was placed under imperial ban and translated the New Testament into German while sheltered at the Wartburg. He later championed justification by faith, marriage for clergy and worship in the vernacular, and criticized the violence of the Peasants' War. He died in 1546. Luther's protest triggered the Reformation, shattered the unity of Latin Christendom and promoted standard German, literacy and state churches; his attitudes toward Jews and peasants also left a contested legacy. His German Bible was not the only translation, but it spread rapidly through printing and changed religious reading habits in Europe. His Bible translation and catechisms let ordinary people read religious texts directly and also produced new forms of authority.
