Kublai Khan

Person
Recorded range · 1251 CE — 1294 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 忽必烈 / 元世祖

Kublai Khan (1215–1294) was born in Mongolia, a grandson of Genghis Khan and son of Tolui. After Möngke's death in 1259 he fought his brother Ariq Böke for the great khanate and was enthroned at Kaiping in 1260. In 1271 he proclaimed the dynastic name Yuan, in 1272 fixed the capital at Dadu and in 1279 destroyed the Southern Song, completing the unification of China. He retained Mongol appanages and the kesig guard while adopting Chinese-style bureaucracy, provinces and paper currency, producing a mixed Mongol-Chinese system. Abroad, his two expeditions against Japan and campaigns in Vietnam and Java all failed; at home he employed Central Asian merchants, built canals and post stations, and faced fiscal inflation and ethnic hierarchy. He died in 1294. Kublai's Yuan connected the steppe empire with agrarian China, but his rule oscillated between Mongol tradition and Chinese institutions. He issued the Zhongtong paper currency and built post stations, making the Yuan a hub of trade and information between Europe and Asia. After his death the Yuan continued to expand, but fiscal and ethnic tensions accelerated its decline.
