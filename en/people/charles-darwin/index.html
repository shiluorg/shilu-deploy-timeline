Charles Darwin

Person
Recorded range · 1809 CE — 1882 CE5 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 达尔文 / 查尔斯·达尔文

Charles Darwin (1809–1882) was born in Shrewsbury, England, and studied at Edinburgh and Cambridge, initially preparing for medicine or the clergy. From 1831 to 1836 he sailed around the world on HMS Beagle, collecting plants, animals and geological specimens in South America, Australia and the Pacific islands, and began to doubt that species were fixed. After his return he organized specimens and studied pigeons and barnacles, gradually formulating the theory of natural selection. In 1859 he published On the Origin of Species, explaining evolution through variation, inheritance, competition and adaptation; in 1871 The Descent of Man placed humans within the same framework. Darwin did not possess modern genetics, but his questions and organization of evidence transformed biology. His theory also provoked religious and ethical controversy and remains foundational for understanding the diversity of life. He delayed publication for nearly two decades, partly because the evidence was incomplete and partly from fear of religious reaction; Wallace's independent discovery prompted him to publish hurriedly. On the Origin of Species organized evidence from artificial selection, the geological record and geographical distribution rather than presenting a single abstract principle.
