Saladin

Person
Recorded range · 1169 CE — 1193 CE9 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 萨拉丁 / 萨拉丁·阿尤布

Saladin (1137–1193) was born in Tikrit of Kurdish descent and early served the Zengid dynasty, accompanying his uncle Shirkuh on campaigns in Egypt. In 1169 he became the effective ruler of Egypt, abolished the Fatimid caliphate in 1171 and founded the Ayyubid dynasty. He integrated Egypt, Syria and Yemen and replaced Fatimid Ismailism with Sunni institutions. In 1187 he defeated the army of the Kingdom of Jerusalem at Hattin and recaptured Jerusalem, triggering the Third Crusade. He confronted Richard I for years and concluded a treaty in 1192 that allowed Christian pilgrimage. He died in Damascus in 1193. Saladin's image as a chivalrous sultan rests on military alliances, religious legitimacy and relatively generous treatment of captives, but his unification depended on personal relationships and the Ayyubid realm fragmented soon after his death. After his death the Ayyubid family fragmented and the Mamluks eventually replaced its dynasty. He invested in scholarship and religious endowments and built schools and hospitals, linking military victory with institutional reconstruction.
