Wu Zetian

Person
Recorded range · 654 CE — 685 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 武则天 / 则天大圣皇帝

Wu Zetian (624–705) was born in Lizhou, entered Emperor Taizong's palace at fourteen and later became Emperor Gaozong's empress. As Gaozong's health declined she participated in government and was called “Two Sages” with him; after Zhongzong ascended in 683 she ruled from behind the curtain and deposed Zhongzong and Ruizong. In 690 she changed the dynastic name to Zhou and took the title of emperor, the only woman in Chinese history to reign formally as emperor. She attacked the Guanlong aristocracy, promoted examination candidates and lesser families, built the Mingtang and Buddhist temples, and used harsh officials to consolidate power. In her last years Zhang Yizhi and Zhang Changzong interfered in politics; she was forced to abdicate in the Shenlong coup of 705 and died the same year. Wu Zetian broadened recruitment but paid for it with denunciation, collective punishment and palace intrigue; her gender kept later evaluations oscillating between “female calamity” and “great talent.” She built legitimacy through examinations, Buddhism and script reform and elevated Luoyang as a political center. Her rule prepared talent and institutions for the Kaiyuan flourishing age, but the cost of palace struggle was equally deep. After her abdication Zhongzong returned to the throne and the Tang dynastic name was restored.
