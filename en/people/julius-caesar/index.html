Julius Caesar

Person
Recorded range · 100 BCE — 49 BCE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 凯撒 / 恺撒 / Gaius Julius Caesar

Julius Caesar (100–44 BCE) was born into a Roman noble family and early combined military service with politics, serving as priest, quaestor and consul. In 60 BCE he formed the First Triumvirate with Pompey and Crassus; from 58 to 50 BCE he conquered Gaul, pushing Roman territory to the Rhine and the English Channel and accumulating troops, wealth and prestige. In 49 BCE he crossed the Rubicon and precipitated civil war; in 48 BCE he defeated Pompey at Pharsalus and in 46 BCE became dictator for life. He reformed the calendar, granted citizenship in the provinces, settled veterans and weakened the Senate. On 15 March 44 BCE he was assassinated in the Senate by Brutus and others. Caesar's rise showed that the Republic's institutions could not contain a trans-regional military strongman; his name later became the title of emperor. His Gallic War is both a military report and political propaganda and shaped later impressions of the conquest. He left no stable succession arrangement; after the assassination civil war resumed and Octavian eventually established the principate.
