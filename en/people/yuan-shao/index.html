Yuan Shao

Person
Recorded range · 188 CE — 202 CE10 event anchors1 storiesAlso recorded as 袁绍 / 本初

Yuan Shao (154–202), courtesy name Benchu, was a native of Ruyang in Runan and came from the Yuan family that had produced four generations of three-excellency offices. At the end of the Eastern Han he raised troops against Dong Zhuo and was elected leader of the eastern coalition, but the allies pursued their own interests and failed to follow up. He held Ji, Qing, You and Bing provinces and became the strongest power in the north, yet at Guandu he was indecisive, rejected the protracted-war advice of Ju Shou and Tian Feng and lost the Wuchao grain depot, after which his army collapsed. He died in 202 and his sons divided, allowing Cao Cao to absorb Hebei. Yuan Shao's failure is often reduced to “many plans, few decisions,” but more deeply his coalition of great families could not sustain long war mobilization; his regime showed that prestige and territory do not equal executive capacity. He relied on Yuan family clients and Hebei magnates, but his internal factions split openly around Guandu. His defeat gave Cao Cao Hebei's grain, manpower and gentry support. Yuan Shao's failure also shows that local coalitions at the end of the Eastern Han required continuous logistics and personnel integration.
