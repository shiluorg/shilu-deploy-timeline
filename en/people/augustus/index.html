Augustus

Person
Recorded range · 63 BCE — 18 BCE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 奥古斯都 / 屋大维 / Octavian

Augustus (63 BCE–14 CE), born Octavian, came from a Roman equestrian family and was Caesar's great-nephew and adopted son. After Caesar's assassination in 44 BCE he formed the Second Triumvirate with Antony and Lepidus, defeated the republican forces at Philippi in 42 BCE and defeated Antony and Cleopatra at Actium in 31 BCE. In 27 BCE he returned his powers to the Senate and accepted the title Augustus while retaining control of the army, provinces and finances, creating the principate. He reorganized taxation, established standing and praetorian forces: rebuilt Rome, promoted moral and marriage legislation, and presented personal rule as peace and order. He died in 14 CE and was succeeded by Tiberius. Augustus did not restore the Republic; he institutionalized the military power produced by the civil wars into an inheritable imperial office. His successors retained the title of princeps and the legionary system, giving Rome relative stability for nearly two centuries, but imperial succession never acquired clear rules. The principate kept the assemblies and republican offices as ceremonial shells while real decisions moved to the emperor and his household.
