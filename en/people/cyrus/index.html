Cyrus

Person
Recorded range · 559 BCE — 539 BCE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 居鲁士 / 居鲁士大帝

Cyrus (c. 600–530 BCE) was born into the Persian Achaemenid clan and became king of Persia in 559 BCE. He first conquered Media, combining Persian and Median elites, then Lydia and western Asia Minor; in 539 BCE he entered Babylon and inherited the Neo-Babylonian empire. He allowed Babylonian priests and exiles to restore local cults, and later traditions made him a symbol of tolerant rule; imperial expansion nevertheless brought war, tribute and cooperation with local elites. The provincial and satrapal system attributed to Cyrus laid foundations for the administration of Darius. He died campaigning in Central Asia in 530 BCE and was buried at Pasargadae. Persian, Greek and Jewish traditions each shaped his image into different stories. The Cyrus Cylinder has been given different symbolic meanings in modern Iran and at the United Nations, showing how ancient documents are repeatedly reinterpreted. His empire had no single language and was held together by local temples, roads and tribute.
