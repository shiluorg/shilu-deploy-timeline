Darius

Person
Recorded range · 522 BCE — 488 BCE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 大流士 / 大流士一世

Darius (c. 550–486 BCE) was born into a Persian noble family and seized the throne in 522 BCE as Darius I. He suppressed revolts across the empire and recorded the process in the Behistun inscription, proclaiming his legitimacy in three languages. He divided the empire into provinces, appointed satraps, standardized coinage and taxation, built the royal road from Susa to Sardis and began the palace at Persepolis. The empire was held together by central finance, local elites and road networks, but his expedition against Greece ended in defeat at Marathon in 490 BCE. He died in 486 BCE and was succeeded by Xerxes. Darius's administrative legacy mattered more than his conquests: he turned a multi-ethnic empire into a structure that could be taxed, supplied and garrisoned. The Behistun inscription later became a key to reading cuneiform and lets us see how the emperor narrated rebellion. He balanced satraps against royal auditors and garrisons, so provinces could absorb local traditions while still sending revenue to the center.
