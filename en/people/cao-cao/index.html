Cao Cao

Person
Recorded range · 187 CE — 202 CE10 event anchors6 storiesAlso recorded as 曹操 / 孟德 / Cao Mengde

Cao Cao (155–220), courtesy name Mengde, was a native of Qiao in Pei and a military and political leader at the end of the Han dynasty as well as a representative of Jian'an literature. As a young man he was known for chivalric behavior; at twenty he was recommended as filial and incorrupt and later served as commandant of the northern district of Luoyang and as chancellor of Jinan, where he imposed strict law and order. In 196 he brought Emperor Xian to Xu and gained the political formula of acting in the emperor's name; in 200 he defeated Yuan Shao at Guandu and gradually unified the north. In 208 he moved south into Jing province but was defeated by the allied forces of Sun Quan and Liu Bei at Red Cliffs, establishing the north-south division. He subsequently promoted military farming, waterworks and merit-based recruitment and was enfeoffed as Duke and then King of Wei, creating a separate principality. He died in 220 and Cao Pi replaced Han with Wei. Cao Cao was skilled at converting military victory into institutions and personnel, but his regime depended on war mobilization and placed heavy burdens on local society and the elite. His military farming and merit-based recruitment prepared the material and personnel basis for Wei and Jin unification, while ruling through the emperor left him with a long reputation as a usurper. Soon after his death Cao Pi took the imperial title, showing that Cao Cao had already prepared the way for hereditary power.
