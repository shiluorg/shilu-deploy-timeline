Kangxi

Person
Recorded range · 1661 CE — 1691 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 康熙 / 玄烨 / 清圣祖

The Kangxi Emperor (1654–1722), personal name Xuanye, was the third son of the Shunzhi Emperor and came to the throne in 1661 under the regency of Oboi and others. After taking personal rule in 1669 he arrested Oboi, suppressed the Three Feudatories, recovered Taiwan, resisted Russia and concluded the Treaty of Nerchinsk, and campaigned repeatedly against the Dzungars, drawing Mongolia and Tibet into a tighter tributary and garrison system. He patronized Confucian learning, commissioned the Kangxi Dictionary and the Gujin Tushu Jicheng and employed missionaries to study astronomy, cartography and medicine. In his later years the struggle among his sons weakened succession and administration, leaving fiscal and frontier problems. He died in 1722 and was succeeded by Yongzheng. Kangxi turned the Qing from a post-conquest military regime into a multi-ethnic empire, but his success depended on personal diligence and cooperation with the Manchu military elite, and the succession remained a structural weakness. The national survey behind the Huangyu Quanlan Tu combined missionary techniques with Qing knowledge of its territory. He was deeply interested in Western science in later life but did not turn calendar and mathematical reform into a nationwide educational system.

    Shunzhi Emperor dies; Kangxi Emperor ascends

    On February 5, the Shunzhi Emperor died of smallpox at age 23. His will designated his third son Xuanye as the Kangxi Emperor, with four regents: Sonin, Suksaha, Ebilun, and Oboi. Kangxi was only 8 years old.
