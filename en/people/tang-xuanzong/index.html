Tang Xuanzong

Person
Recorded range · 710 CE — 742 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 唐玄宗 / 李隆基 / 唐明皇

Emperor Xuanzong of Tang (685–762), personal name Li Longji, was the third son of Ruizong. In 710 he and Princess Taiping staged a coup and installed Ruizong; he took the throne in 712 under the Kaiyuan era. In his early reign he employed Yao Chong and Song Jing, reformed administration, cut redundant offices and promoted economic growth, producing the narrative of the Kaiyuan flourishing age. Later he neglected government, favored Li Linfu and Yang Guozhong and allowed frontier military governors to grow too powerful; the An Lushan rebellion broke out in 755, Luoyang and Chang'an fell, he fled to Sichuan and abdicated as retired emperor in 756. He died in 762. Xuanzong both created the peak of Tang and pushed the empire into long-term warlord fragmentation through institutional imbalance and personnel failure; his story with Yang Guifei was later literaryized as a metaphor for dynastic rise and fall. He reformed the military, finance and examinations early in his reign but later handed frontier armies to military governors, creating a strong periphery and weak center. After the An Lushan rebellion Tang recovered somewhat, but warlord provinces, eunuchs and factional strife became long-term structures. His music and the Pear Garden institution also influenced later Chinese theater.
