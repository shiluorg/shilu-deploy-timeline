Qin Shi Huang

Person
Recorded range · 259 BCE — 210 BCE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 秦始皇 / 嬴政

Qin Shi Huang (259–210 BCE), personal name Zheng, was born in Handan, became king of Qin at thirteen and took personal rule in 238 BCE after suppressing the factions of Lao Ai and Lü Buwei. From 230 to 221 BCE he conquered Han, Zhao, Wei, Chu, Yan and Qi in sequence, founded the Qin dynasty and took the title First Emperor. He imposed commandery and county administration, standardized writing, weights, measures, coinage and axle widths, built roads, walls, the Epang Palace and his mausoleum, and burned books and buried scholars to suppress criticism. He died on a tour in 210 BCE; Zhao Gao and Li Si forged his succession decree, the Second Emperor took the throne and Qin quickly collapsed. Qin Shi Huang institutionalized the centralizing trends of the Warring States, but his rule exhausted society through harsh law, labor service and massive construction and became the core of later narratives of tyranny. His mausoleum and terracotta army were systematically excavated after 1974 and became key archaeological evidence for Qin military organization and craft. After unification he toured the realm repeatedly and inscribed steles to celebrate his achievements, trying to shape imperial authority through text and ritual.
