Buddha

Person
Recorded range · 563 BCE — 147 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 释迦牟尼 / 佛陀 / 乔达摩·悉达多

The Buddha, Siddhartha Gautama, was born around the fifth century BCE in Kapilavastu into the Shakya clan. Traditional accounts say that after encountering birth, aging, sickness and death he left the palace, practiced severe asceticism and finally awakened under the Bodhi tree. His teaching centered on the Four Noble Truths, the Eightfold Path, dependent origination and non-self, explaining the origin of suffering and its cessation while rejecting the idea that lineage or sacrifice alone could bring liberation. After his awakening he taught in the Ganges valley and founded a monastic community that admitted people of different castes and genders; he died at Kushinagar at about eighty. He left no systematic writings, and his teachings were transmitted through communal recitation and later developed into Theravada, Mahasanghika, Mahayana and tantric traditions. His thought was both a religion and a response to Brahmanical order and the broader ascetic movements of his time. Later councils and debates produced different canonical languages and paths of practice, and Buddhism spread through Central Asia into China, Southeast Asia and Tibet. His teaching emphasized practice and observation rather than substituting metaphysical speculation for liberation. He rejected caste lineage as the determinant of spiritual attainment, and the monastic community was ordered by precepts, study and debate.
