Galileo

Person
Recorded range · 1564 CE — 1633 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 伽利略 / 伽利略·伽利雷

Galileo (1564–1642) was born in Pisa, began medical studies at the University of Pisa and turned to mathematics and natural philosophy. He studied falling bodies, pendulums and projectiles and insisted on describing nature through experiment and mathematics. In 1609 he used an improved telescope to observe the lunar surface, Jupiter's moons and the phases of Venus, providing new visible evidence for heliocentrism and bringing him into conflict with supporters of the Ptolemaic system. He published the Dialogue on the Two Chief World Systems in 1632, was tried by the Inquisition the following year, forced to recant heliocentrism and placed under house arrest. He continued writing in later life and organized results in mechanics and kinematics. Galileo did not complete a unified physical system, but his combination of instruments, observation and mathematical argument made him a central symbol of modern scientific method. His trial became a symbol of the conflict between science and authority in the Enlightenment, though voices inside the church also supported his mathematical work. His late Two New Sciences summarized falling bodies and the strength of materials, providing problems and tools for Newton's generation.
