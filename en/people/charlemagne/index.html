Charlemagne

Person
Recorded range · 742 CE — 778 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 查理曼 / 查理大帝

Charlemagne (742–814) was a ruler of the Frankish Carolingian dynasty, ruling jointly with his brother from 768 and alone from 771. He conquered the Lombards in 774 and gained northern Italy, then fought more than three decades of war in Saxony, using forced conversion, deportation and frontier marches to secure control, and intervened across the Pyrenees in the Spanish march. In 800 Pope Leo III crowned him emperor of the Romans in Rome, reconnecting Frankish kingship with the western church. He developed counts, margraves and missi dominici, supported monasteries and palace schools and attracted scholars such as Alcuin, a period often called the Carolingian Renaissance. After his death in 814 the empire passed to Louis the Pious and was divided by the Treaty of Verdun in 843. Charlemagne's empire rested on personal prestige and cooperation with the church and never established a stable succession system. The Carolingian minuscule and monastic copying promoted under his rule preserved many classical texts and provided a basis for later European written culture.
