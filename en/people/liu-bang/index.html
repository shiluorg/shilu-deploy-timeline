Liu Bang

Person
Recorded range · 208 BCE — 195 BCE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 刘邦 / 汉高祖 / 汉高帝

Liu Bang (256–195 BCE) was born in Fengyi in Pei county and served as a local magistrate of the Sishui pavilion, learning the routines of local officials and common people. After Chen Sheng's uprising at the end of Qin, he raised troops in Pei, entered the Guanzhong region first, accepted the surrender of the Qin heir and agreed with local elders on a simple three-point law. In the Chu-Han war he used Guanzhong as his base and allied with Han Xin, Peng Yue and Ying Bu, finally defeating Xiang Yu at Gaixia and founding the Han dynasty at Chang'an in 202 BCE. After taking the throne he eliminated the non-royal kings, enfeoffed relatives and promoted light taxes and recovery; after the Baideng siege he adopted a marriage alliance with the Xiongnu. He died in 195 BCE. Liu Bang's success combined Qin commandery administration, a military merit group and local strongmen, but enfeoffing relatives planted the seeds of the later Rebellion of the Seven States. The combination of “Han continuing Qin institutions” with parallel commanderies and kingdoms became a governance template debated by later dynasties. In his later years he governed alongside Empress Lü, and the balance between the imperial relatives and the founding generals became the main line of early Han politics. After his death, Emperor Hui succeeded and Empress Lü ruled from behind the curtain.
