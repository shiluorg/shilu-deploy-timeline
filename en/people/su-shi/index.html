Su Shi

Person
Recorded range · 1050 CE — 1094 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 苏轼 / 苏东坡 / 东坡居士

Su Shi (1037–1101), courtesy name Zizhan and styled Dongpo, was a native of Meishan in Meizhou. He passed the jinshi examination in 1057 and, opposing the radical methods of Wang Anshi's New Policies, was repeatedly sent to provincial posts in Hangzhou, Mizhou, Xuzhou and Huzhou. In 1079 the Wutai poetry case led to his exile in Huangzhou, where he wrote the Red Cliff prose poem and “Niannu Jiao: Remembering the Past at Red Cliff,” developing a broad and concrete literary style. He later rose and fell again between the Hanlin Academy, provincial office and southern exile, was pardoned in old age and died in Changzhou in 1101. Su Shi was innovative in poetry, ci, prose, calligraphy and painting and also worked on water conservancy, medicine and local government. His significance is not merely genius but the way he turned political failure into aesthetic and ethical experience in everyday life. At Huangzhou he farmed the eastern slope and wrote exile life as a record of daily labor and friendship rather than pure political complaint. His poetry excels in concrete objects and everyday experience, and later generations treated him as a representative of Song literati culture.
