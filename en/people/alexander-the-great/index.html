Alexander the Great

Person
Recorded range · 518 BCE — 323 BCE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 亚历山大大帝 / 亚历山大

Alexander the Great (356–323 BCE) was born in the Macedonian royal court; his father Philip II unified Greece and reformed the army, and Aristotle served as his tutor. After Philip's assassination in 336 BCE, Alexander took the throne, suppressed revolts in the Greek cities and crossed into Asia in 334 BCE to campaign against Persia. He advanced through Asia Minor, Syria, Egypt and Mesopotamia, defeated Darius III at Gaugamela and then moved into Central Asia and the Indus valley. He died in Babylon in 323 BCE, and his empire soon fragmented into the successor kingdoms of the Ptolemies, Seleucids and Antigonids. Alexander did not build a stable bureaucracy, but city foundations, coinage, the Greek language and military networks connected the Mediterranean to Central Asia; his conquest brought destruction and enslavement as well as the long exchanges of the Hellenistic world. The wars of the successors lasted for decades and eventually produced Hellenistic kingdoms that combined Greek cities, royal houses and local elites. Those kingdoms later confronted Rome and became lasting parts of the political map of the eastern Mediterranean.
