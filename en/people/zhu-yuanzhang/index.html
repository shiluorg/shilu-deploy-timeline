Zhu Yuanzhang

Person
Recorded range · 1351 CE — 1365 CE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 朱元璋 / 洪武帝 / 明太祖

Zhu Yuanzhang (1328–1398) was born in Zhongli, Haozhou, to a poor peasant family and became a monk at the Huangjue temple as a youth. After the Red Turban uprising at the end of the Yuan he joined Guo Zixing's forces, gradually became independent and followed Zhu Sheng's advice to “build high walls, store grain widely and delay claiming kingship.” In 1368 he declared himself emperor at Yingtian, founded the Ming dynasty and adopted the Hongwu era. He promoted military farming, land surveys and household tax registration, reformed administration and purged founding generals in the Hu Weiyong and Lan Yu cases, abolishing the chancellorship and concentrating imperial power. He enfeoffed his sons as princes to guard the dynasty, planting the seeds of the Jingnan war. He died in 1398 and was succeeded by the Jianwen emperor. Zhu Yuanzhang restored Han rule but also gave Ming politics its harsh, centralized and secret-police character. He compiled the Great Ming Code and the Great Warnings, punishing corrupt officials harshly and encouraging commoners to denounce them. After abolishing the chancellorship, the emperor handled the six ministries directly, and the workload grew so much that the later Grand Secretariat gradually filled the gap. The princes he enfeoffed later became a fiscal burden and the military base of the Jingnan war.
