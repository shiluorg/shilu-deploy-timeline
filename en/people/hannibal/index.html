Hannibal

Person
Recorded range · 233 BCE — 216 BCE10 event anchors0 storiesAlso recorded as 汉尼拔 / 汉尼拔·巴卡

Hannibal (247–183 BCE) was born in Carthage into the Barca family; his father Hamilcar had operated in Spain during the First Punic War. When the Second Punic War broke out, Hannibal led an army from Spain across the Alps into Italy and repeatedly defeated Roman legions through cavalry and terrain. At Cannae in 216 BCE he surrounded and destroyed a large Roman force, a classic of ancient tactics; Rome refused to sue for peace and instead attacked Carthaginian bases in Spain and North Africa. At Zama in 202 BCE Hannibal was defeated by Scipio, and Carthage accepted a treaty. He later went into exile in the Seleucid and Bithynian courts and took poison in 183 BCE. Hannibal excelled at tactical maneuver but could not convert victories into a strategic end, one of the most famous dilemmas in ancient warfare. His Alpine march and Cannae tactics have been studied repeatedly in military academies, but Carthaginian sea power and supply never matched his strategy. After his death Rome destroyed Carthage completely, completing the transfer of Mediterranean hegemony.
