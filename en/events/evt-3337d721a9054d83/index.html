Around AD 37, Kujula Kadphises, chief of the Kushan tribe (one of the five Yuezhi Xihou), united the other four tribes, established the Kushan Empire in the Bactria region, and formally proclaimed himself 'King of Kings', establishing his capital at Bactra (modern Balkh, Afghanistan). He issued coins in the Roman style and amassed vast wealth through Silk Road trade. At its peak, the Kushan Empire encompassed parts of modern Afghanistan, Pakistan, and northern India.

Kujula’s royal title marked the Kushans’ move from one clan in the Yuezhi confederation to a polity with a formal kingship. Title, coin and city were the minimal toolkit for turning a clan coalition into a state.
