Kushan Buddhist art spreads to the Chinese Western Regions

Under Kanishka I, Kushan Buddhist art reached its zenith. Numerous Buddha and Bodhisattva statues and sutras were transmitted via the southern Silk Road to Western Regions kingdoms such as Khotan, Kashgar, and Kucha. Artisans there imitated Gandharan styles, carving the first cave temples (e.g., Kizil Caves) and painting Buddhist murals with Hellenistic features. This Buddhist art later provided models for the Mogao Caves and Yungang Grottoes, marking a significant starting point for the Sinification of Buddhism.

Buddhist art entered China through the Western Regions via monks, caravans and oasis cities. A religious route was also a route of translation, patronage and pilgrimage.
