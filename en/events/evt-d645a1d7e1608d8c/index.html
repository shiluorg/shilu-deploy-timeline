Ashurbanipal sent scholars to Babylonia to copy ancient texts, collecting tens of thousands of clay tablets containing epics, science, and religion, preserving much of Mesopotamian knowledge for posterity.

Ashurbanipal’s library collected divination, medicine, literature and administrative texts. It preserved the Epic of Gilgamesh and made “collecting the world’s knowledge” a display of imperial power.

Ashurbanipal sent scholars to Babylonia to copy ancient documents and collected tens of thousands of clay tablets, including epics, science, and religion, and preserved a large amount of Mesopotamian civilization knowledge for future generations.1 The sending of scholars out to copy indicates that the library was not a passive collection, but an organized copy of knowledge traditions from other places.2 The juxtaposition of epic, scientific and religious texts in the collection also shows that the royal family tried to incorporate different types of knowledge into the same set of palace archives.
