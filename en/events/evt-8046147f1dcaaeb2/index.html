On June 7, 1494, Spain and Portugal, mediated by the papacy, signed the Treaty of Tordesillas, fixing the line of demarcation 370 leagues west of the Cape Verde islands (about 46° W longitude). The treaty granted Portugal all lands east and Spain all lands west, essentially dividing the non-European world into two colonial empires. Brazil later became Portuguese because of this treaty.

The Treaty of Tordesillas divided the world between Spain and Portugal before accurate maps existed. It shows that “discovery” was from the start an inter-state accounting exercise, not a private adventure.
