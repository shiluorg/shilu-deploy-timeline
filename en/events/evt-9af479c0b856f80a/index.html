Emperor Xian of Han appoints Cao Cao as King of Wei, ranking above all feudal kings

Cao Cao: From Chancellor of Jinan to King of Wei
Chapter 8 of 8
Previous chapterEnfeoffed Duke of Wei

In 216, under pressure, Emperor Xian appointed Cao Cao King of Wei, his status higher than all other feudal kings, with imperial regalia. The event record lists a twelve-pendant crown, a gold root-carriage chariot, a six-horse team, and five-colored secondary chariots.

Cao Cao established his royal capital at Yecheng, set up an ancestral temple, and named Cao Pi crown prince of Wei. The record states that although he still nominally called himself a Han minister, he was de facto emperor, and the Eastern Han existed in name only.

This chapter caps Cao Cao's path to power: from Chancellor of Jinan to Duke and King of Wei, the fief institutions were completed step by step. The record does not describe the later arrangements for replacing the Han; the succession under Cao Pi is covered in related stories.
