Zheng He's fifth voyage reaches East Africa

In May 1417 Zheng He's fleet began its fifth voyage, escorting envoys from nineteen states home. The fleet called at Champa, Java, Pahang, Palembang, Malacca, Sumatra, Ceylon, Kollam and Calicut; detached squadrons sailed to the Maldives, Mogadishu, Barawa, Malindi, Dhofar, Aden, Lasa and Hormuz. It returned on 8 August 1419.

The Ming History dates this voyage to the "nineteenth year, spring" and the return to the "following year, eighth month", which converts to 1421–1422; the common numbering places it in 1417–1419. The two reckonings differ by two years, and this story keeps the difference instead of merging them.

The voyage placed the ports of the western Indian Ocean and the Arabian Peninsula within one language of gifts and tribute. The detached squadrons were not "side expeditions"; they allowed the fleet to reach several ports within different monsoon windows.

Sources: CCTV, "The main countries and regions Zheng He's seven voyages passed through, and their dates," fifth voyage section (departure May 1417, nineteen states, the three East African ports, return 8 August 1419); Ming History, "Biography of Zheng He," entries from the nineteenth year, spring, to the following year, eighth month (the dating difference).
