Zheng He's fourth voyage reaches Hormuz

In the winter of 1413 Zheng He's fleet began its fourth voyage. It sailed via Champa, Java, Palembang, Malacca, Sumatra, Ceylon and Calicut, then directly from Calicut to Hormuz, returning on 12 August 1415.

Hormuz was a commercial centre at the mouth of the Persian Gulf. There the fleet encountered local merchants and information about markets further west. The Ming History records that in the winter of the fourteenth year envoys from Malacca, Calicut and nineteen other states came to court, and Zheng He was sent back with them to give gifts to their rulers, returning in the seventh month of the seventeenth year.

This voyage is often described as "reaching East Africa", but putting another detached squadron's destination into this voyage mixes two expeditions together; this story keeps them separate.

Hormuz mattered less as a "furthest point" than as an entry into the commercial language of the Persian Gulf. Ming gifts and patents entered a network already maintained by local merchants; the fleet did not build a new order from zero.

Sources: CCTV, "The main countries and regions Zheng He's seven voyages passed through, and their dates," fourth voyage section (departure winter 1413, direct passage from Calicut to Hormuz, return 12 August 1415); Ming History, "Biography of Zheng He," entries from the winter of the fourteenth year to the seventh month of the seventeenth year (the nineteen states).
