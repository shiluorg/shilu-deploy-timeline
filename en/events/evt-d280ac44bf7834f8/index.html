Foundation of the University of Cambridge

Event
1209 CEEnglandEducationLegacy import · pending source reviewL1 · Structure checked

Volume position: Frankish & Medieval Europe · Ch. 4/8

Locate on the globe ↗Compare civilizations ↗More in this year ↗

In 1209 a quarrel between Oxford scholars and townsmen ended with two clerks (some say three) hanged by the town authorities, without benefit of ecclesiastical trial, for the alleged killing of a woman.1,2,3,4,5 The Oxford masters suspended teaching and dispersed; a group moved east to Cambridge on the River Cam, lecturing in churches such as St Benet's and gradually forming an independent guild of scholars.6 A chancellor is recorded from 1225; in 1231 Henry III confirmed the scholars' privileges and granted them a monopoly of teaching, and in 1233 Pope Gregory IX recognized the validity of their degrees, making Cambridge the second university of the English-speaking world.7 It followed Oxford's faculties—theology, canon and civil law, medicine and arts.8 In 1284 Hugh de Balsham, bishop of Ely, founded Peterhouse, the first college, and the collegiate system became the distinctive form of both English universities.9 In modern times Cambridge has been pre-eminent in the sciences, counting Newton, Darwin, Maxwell and Rutherford among its members.10
