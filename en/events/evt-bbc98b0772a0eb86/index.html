Original entry and sentence-level review

On March 24, following his victory at the Battle of Sekigahara (1600), Tokugawa Ieyasu was appointed shogun by Emperor Go-Yōzei and opened his shogunate in Edo (present-day Tokyo).1 This marked the end of Japan's Warring States period and the start of the Edo period (Tokugawa shogunate).2 Ieyasu later solidified control over the daimyō through the 'Laws for the Military Houses' and the 'alternate attendance' system, leading to 265 years of peace and isolation.3
