Emperor Xian of Han appoints Cao Cao as Duke of Wei, establishes state of Wei, grants ten commanderies

In 213, under pressure from Cao Cao, Emperor Xian of Han issued an edict appointing Cao Cao Duke of Wei, granting him the Nine Bestowals, establishing a Wei bureaucracy, and giving him ten Jizhou commanderies as fief with the capital at Yecheng.

The event record notes this was the first time in the Eastern Han that a man not of the imperial clan was made a duke; the only earlier case was Wang Mang as Duke of Anhan. The Duchy of Wei signified that Cao Cao had formally separated from the Han bureaucracy, with his own ancestral temple, secretariat, and officials, taking a decisive step toward replacing the Han.

In the person-centered line, this chapter is an institutional turning point: before it, Cao Cao issued orders through Han offices; after it, he held a fief with its own system. The record does not detail the administration of the ten commanderies or the roster of officials.

Under pressure from Cao Cao, Emperor Xian of Han issued an edict appointing Cao Cao as Duke of Wei, granting him the Nine Bestowals, and establishing a Wei bureaucracy.1 Ten commanderies of Jizhou were given as his fief, with the capital at Yecheng.2 This was the first time in the Eastern Han that a man not of the imperial clan was made a duke (the only previous case was Wang Mang as Duke of Anhan).3 The establishment of the Duchy of Wei signified that Cao Cao had formally separated himself from the Han bureaucracy, with his own ancestral temple, imperial secretariat, and bureaucracy, taking a decisive step toward replacing the Han.4
