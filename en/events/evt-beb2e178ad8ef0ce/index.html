Pasteur develops anthrax vaccine

Event
1881 CEFranceMedicineLegacy import · pending source reviewL2 · Source linked

Volume position: Frankish & Medieval Europe · Ch. 1/8

Locate on the globe ↗Compare civilizations ↗More in this year ↗

In 1881 Louis Pasteur developed an anthrax vaccine.1 He tested it in a public trial at Pouilly-le-Fort. The vaccinated group comprised 24 sheep, 1 goat and 6 cows; the control group comprised 24 sheep, 1 goat and 4 cows. The vaccinated sheep and goat survived exposure to a virulent anthrax culture, while all 24 unvaccinated sheep and the unvaccinated goat died.2 The unvaccinated cows did not die, but they developed marked swelling and fever, whereas the vaccinated cows remained healthy.3

The trial showed that a prepared vaccine could train the body to resist a lethal disease, giving artificial immunity a practical demonstration. Its design—a vaccinated group, a control group and an outcome open to witnesses—became a template for later public vaccine trials.
