From roughly 1400 to 400 BCE, the Olmec civilization flourished along the Gulf Coast of Mexico (present‑day Veracruz and Tabasco). The Olmec carved colossal basalt heads (up to 3 metres high and 20‑50 tonnes), developed a calendar and the earliest writing in Mesoamerica, and worshipped the jaguar god and the feathered serpent. San Lorenzo and La Venta served as political and religious centres. The Olmecs are recognised as the 'mother culture' of Mesoamerica, influencing the Maya, Zapotec, and Aztec civilisations.

The Olmec are often called the “mother civilisation,” a term that compresses a complex region into one origin. More cautiously, they supplied a shareable language of symbols, ritual and art.
