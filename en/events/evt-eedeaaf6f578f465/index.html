Solomon's Temple completed, Ark brought in

After seven years, the First Temple was completed. King Solomon presided over a grand dedication ceremony, moving the Ark of the Covenant from the City of David to the Temple's inner sanctuary. The priests sacrificed 120,000 cattle and sheep, and the entire nation celebrated for fourteen days. The Temple became the spiritual symbol of Israel and the pilgrimage center for the three annual festivals.

The Temple’s completion and the transfer of the ark completed a political theology in which God dwelt in the royal capital. Dynastic legitimacy could now be seen, worshipped and taxed.

Original entry and sentence-level review

When the First Temple was completed, the Ark was brought from the City of David into the inner sanctuary, and the dedication lasted fourteen days.1 The Temple was not only a religious building but the centre of royal legitimacy: priests, Levites and the crown controlled the rhythm of sacrifice, and pilgrims brought offerings and revenue.3 Its splendour rested on corvee and taxation, which northern tribes resented; after Solomon's death the kingdom soon split.2 Architectural achievement and political cost have to be read together.
