Battle of Thermopylae

In 480 BCE the Persian king Xerxes I invaded Greece with an army of about 300,000. The Spartan king Leonidas led about 7,000 Greek allies, including 300 Spartan hoplites, to block the Persian advance at the narrow pass of Thermopylae.

After three days of fighting, a local traitor, Ephialtes, showed the Persians a mountain path around the Greek position. Leonidas dismissed the allied troops and stayed with 300 Spartans and several hundred allies to cover the retreat; all of them died. Thermopylae became a symbol of heroic resistance, a defeat that was remembered.

The Chinese and English event records disagree about the identity of this rear guard: one says 700 Thebans, the other 700 Thespians. This chapter does not adjudicate. It keeps only “several hundred allies”. Whether they were Thebans or Thespians, the meaning of Thermopylae lies less in the number than in the time a defeat bought for the Greek coalition.

Persian Emperor Xerxes I invaded Greece with an army of about 300,000.1 Spartan King Leonidas led about 7,000 Greek allies (including 300 Spartan hoplites) to block the Persian advance at the narrow pass of Thermopylae.2 After three days of fierce fighting, a local traitor named Ephialtes showed the Persians a mountain path to outflank the Greeks.3 Leonidas dismissed the allied troops and remained with 300 Spartans and 700 Thespians to cover the retreat; they were all killed.4 Though a defeat, the battle became a symbol of heroic resistance in Western military history.5
