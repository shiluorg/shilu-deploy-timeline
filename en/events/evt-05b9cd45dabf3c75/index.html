Zheng He's seventh voyage and his death at Calicut

On 29 June 1430 the Xuande Emperor ordered Zheng He's seventh voyage. The fleet sailed in 1431, calling at Champa, Java, Palembang, Malacca, Sumatra, Ceylon, Calicut and Hormuz, and returned to Nanjing on 22 July 1433.

Zheng He died at Calicut in the fourth month of 1433, and Wang Jinghong led the fleet home. The Ming History records that in the sixth month of the fifth year of Xuande the emperor, noting that distant states had still not sent tribute, again ordered Zheng He and Wang Jinghong to visit Hormuz and seventeen other states.

"More than thirty states" is the cumulative total of all seven voyages, not the score of the seventh alone. The seventh voyage has fewer clearly recorded ports than the earlier ones; its main task was to restore an interrupted tributary relationship, not to open new routes.

The end of the voyages had more than one cause. A long voyage required the simultaneous coordination of navigation, diplomacy and court resources; after the leading figure died, that coordination was hard to reproduce, and criticism of the cost among civil officials was growing. Reducing all this to "Ming China closed its doors" hides the specific people and accounts involved.

Sources: CCTV, "The main countries and regions Zheng He's seven voyages passed through, and their dates," seventh voyage section (order 29 June 1430, sailing 1431, return to Nanjing 22 July 1433, death at Calicut, Wang Jinghong's return); Ming History, "Biography of Zheng He," entry for the sixth month of the fifth year of Xuande (seventeen states).
