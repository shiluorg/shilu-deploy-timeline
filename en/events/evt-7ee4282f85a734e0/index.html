The Oceanic Turn: From Cape of Good Hope to Circumnavigation
Chapter 2 of 6
Previous chapterDias rounds the Cape of Good HopeNext chapterTreaty of Tordesillas signed

On April 17, 1492, Queen Isabella I of Spain signed the Capitulations of Santa Fe with the Genoese navigator Christopher Columbus, funding his westward voyage to find a route to India and China. On August 3, Columbus set sail from Palos with three ships: the Santa María, the Pinta, and the Niña. On the morning of October 12, lookout Rodrigo de Triana sighted land on an island in the Bahamas (San Salvador). Columbus believed he had reached the East Indies and called the natives "Indians." He brought back gold, spices, and captives, opening sustained European contact with the Americas.

Columbus did not arrive in an empty land. The Caribbean already held Taíno, Kalinago and other societies; the European “discovery” narrative rewrote those people and their land as resources available for possession.
