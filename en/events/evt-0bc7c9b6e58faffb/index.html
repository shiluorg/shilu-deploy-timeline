Zheng He's sixth voyage: escorting envoys from sixteen states

On 3 March 1421 the Yongle Emperor ordered Zheng He to escort envoys from sixteen states home on the sixth voyage. The fleet called at Champa, Siam, Hormuz, Aden, Dhofar, Lasa, Barawa, Mogadishu, Jubba, Malindi, Calicut, Kollam, Kayal, Ceylon, the Maldives, Lambri, Sumatra, Aru, Malacca, Coimbatore and Mombasa, returning on 3 September 1422.

The immediate task was to carry people, not to open a new route. The fleet returned the envoys of sixteen states and brought back envoys from Siam, Sumatra and Aden. The Ming History records only that in the first month of the twenty-second year the chief of Palembang, Shi Jisun, asked to inherit the office of pacification commissioner, and that Zheng He was sent with the seal and patent; by the time he returned, the Yongle Emperor had died.

"Sixteen states" and "more than thirty states" are not the same count: the former refers to the envoys carried on this voyage, the latter to the cumulative destinations of all seven voyages. Mixing the two inflates one voyage into the whole enterprise.

This voyage turned diplomatic escort into routine transport rather than a new geographical discovery. The fleet's main work was to return foreign personnel on schedule and bring the next group of envoys back.

Sources: CCTV, "The main countries and regions Zheng He's seven voyages passed through, and their dates," sixth voyage section (departure 3 March 1421, sixteen states, port list, return 3 September 1422); Ming History, "Biography of Zheng He," entry for the first month of the twenty-second year (Shi Jisun of Palembang).
