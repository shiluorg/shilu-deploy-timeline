In 202 BCE Scipio Africanus and Hannibal met at Zama. This was the decisive battle of the Second Punic War; ancient sources differ on the site, and modern scholars have never precisely identified it.

Hannibal returned from Italy with 12,000 veterans and gathered about 37,000 troops in Africa; Scipio had about 40,000, including 6,100 cavalry commanded by the Numidian king Masinissa and the Roman general Gaius Laelius. Hannibal had only about 4,000 cavalry and 80 poorly trained war elephants.

Scipio arranged his infantry in maniples with broad gaps; when the elephants charged, the soldiers opened lanes and most of the animals passed through without decisive effect. After the Roman cavalry routed the Carthaginian cavalry, Masinissa's Numidian horsemen swung back and attacked Hannibal's veterans from the rear.

Some 20,000 Carthaginians were killed and perhaps 20,000 captured, while the Romans lost about 1,500 dead. After the battle Carthage ceded Spain, surrendered its warships and accepted a 50-year indemnity. Rome became the unchallenged power of the western Mediterranean, but the victory also brought slaves, bullion and provinces back to Italy.
