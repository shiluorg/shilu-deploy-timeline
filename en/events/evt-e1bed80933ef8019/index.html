The Great Pyramid: national logistics in one mountain

Event

The three pyramids of Giza belong to the 4th-dynasty kings Khufu, Khafre and Menkaure; Khufu's Great Pyramid is the largest and oldest of the three.

The Great Pyramid's base averages about 230 metres, its original height was about 147 metres, and it now stands about 138 metres. The stepped stone surface visible today is what remains after the white limestone casing was stripped away.

Khufu's pyramid used about 2.3 million blocks of stone and weighs about 5.75 million tons. Such a mass was not piled up by one gang; it required long-term coordination of quarrying, transport, surveying and rations.

Herodotus says the Great Pyramid took 20 years and 100,000 men; modern archaeological research suggests as few as about 20,000 workers, plus support staff such as bakers and physicians. The two figures differ greatly: the first is an ancient narrative, the second an archaeological estimate.

Workers' districts have been found near the pyramids, including bakeries, storage areas, workshops and the small tombs of artisans.

Writing the pyramids off as 'built by slaves' misses the real capacity: the organisation of quarrying, transport, rations, surveying and seasonal labour.
