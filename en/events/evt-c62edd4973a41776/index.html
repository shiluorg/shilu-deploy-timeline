Sun Quan: A Successor's Vassalage and Imperial Claim
Chapter 4 of 8
Previous chapterMoves capital to Moling, builds Stone CityNext chapterRefuses hostage, adopts era Huangwu

As Liu Bei invaded Wu, Sun Quan declared himself a vassal of Cao Pi to avoid a two-front war and accepted the title King of Wu from Wei. The event record says Cao Pi granted him the Nine Bestowals and appointed him Governor of Jingzhou.

Sun Quan sent Lu Xun, Zhuge Jin, and others to maintain good relations with Wei while secretly building up his strength. The record states that although Cao Pi had hoped to take advantage of the situation to destroy Wu, he hesitated after being advised by Liu Ye and did not act.

In the person-centered line, this chapter shows Sun Quan buying time and security through vassalage: between Shu and Wei, he briefly accepted a tributary standing to avoid pressure from two sides. The record does not detail the bilateral exchanges after the enfeoffment or Wu's internal war preparations.

Liu Bei's invasion of Wu forced Sun Quan to avoid a two‑front war.1 He declared himself a vassal of Cao Pi and accepted the title of King of Wu from Wei, together with the Nine Bestowals and the governorship of Jingzhou.2 Sun Quan sent Lu Xun and Zhuge Jin to maintain good relations with Wei while secretly building up his strength.3 Although Cao Pi had hoped to take advantage of the situation to destroy Wu, he hesitated after being advised by Liu Ye, and did not act.4
