Guan Yu launches the Fancheng campaign, floods seven armies, terrifies the Central Plains

After Liu Bei occupied Hanzhong, Guan Yu set out from Jiangling on the northern campaign, besieging Cao Ren at Fancheng (north of present-day Xiangyang, Hubei). Cao Cao sent Yu Jin and Pang De with about 30,000 men and seven armies to relieve the siege.

In August the Han River flooded. Guan Yu used large warships to attack the Cao camps and flooded the seven armies. Yu Jin surrendered, and Pang De was executed. Guan Yu then besieged Xiangyang, and Cao Cao even considered moving the capital to avoid him. Many counties in the area responded to Guan Yu; this was the peak of his military career.

The northern campaign shook the Central Plains, but the weakness of his rear also stood exposed. The Sun–Liu alliance still held on the surface, yet the unresolved dispute over Jingzhou remained: the stronger the forward push, the more the river defense behind him depended on Eastern Wu's attitude.
