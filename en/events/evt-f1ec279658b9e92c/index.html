Guandu: The Handover of Northern Power

In 200, Cao Cao and Yuan Shao fought their decisive battle at Guandu (present-day Zhongmu, Henan). According to the event record, Yuan Shao had 100,000 troops and ample provisions, while Cao Cao had only 20,000 and was short of supplies. The gap in manpower was clear; on the advice of Xun Yu and Guo Jia, Cao Cao first held his position and waited for the situation to change.

The turning point came from Yuan's own camp. Yuan Shao's adviser Xu You defected to Cao Cao and proposed a raid on the Wuchao grain depot. Cao Cao personally led 5,000 elite cavalry in a night attack and burned Yuan's supplies. With the depot lost, Yuan's army fell into chaos; Cao Cao pressed his attack and, according to the record, destroyed over 70,000 of Yuan's main force. Yuan Shao fled back north across the river with only 800 horsemen.

Guandu was the decisive battle for Cao Cao's unification of the north and a classic case of a smaller force defeating a larger one. The key was not head-on combat but the grain line: with his supplies gone, Yuan's large army disintegrated at once.

Cao Cao and Yuan Shao clashed at Guandu (present-day Zhongmu, Henan) in a decisive battle.1 Yuan Shao had 100,000 troops and ample provisions; Cao Cao had only 20,000 and was short of supplies.2 Cao Cao, following the advice of Xun Yu and Guo Jia, held his position and waited.3 Then Yuan Shao's adviser Xu You defected to Cao Cao and suggested a surprise attack on Yuan Shao's grain depot at Wuchao.4 Cao Cao personally led 5,000 elite cavalry to burn the grain, causing panic in Yuan Shao's army.5 Cao Cao then attacked and destroyed the main Yuan army, killing over 70,000.6 Yuan Shao fled back north across the Yellow River with only 800 horsemen.7 The Battle of Guandu was the decisive battle for Cao Cao's unification of the north, and a classic example of a smaller force defeating a larger one in Chinese history.8
