Lü Meng captures Jiangling in a 'white‑sailed boat' ruse; Guan Yu flees to Maicheng and is killed

Hanzhong: How the Triangle Solidified into Tripartite Rule
Chapter 4 of 7
Previous chapterGuan Yu at Fancheng, flooding the seven armiesNext chapterLiu Bei becomes King of Hanzhong

Eastern Wu commander Lü Meng took advantage of Guan Yu's northern campaign and the weakness in his rear. Disguising his force as merchant ships, he hid his elite soldiers below deck and sailed west day and night, capturing Jiangling and Gong'an by surprise. Mi Fang and Shi Ren surrendered.

When Guan Yu heard the news he turned back, sending envoys to Lü Meng again and again. Lü Meng treated the families of Guan Yu's soldiers well, destroying their morale; Guan Yu's men gradually deserted, and he retreated to Maicheng. Sun Quan tried to persuade him to surrender but failed, then sent Zhu Ran and Pan Zhang to cut off his escape. Guan Yu and his son Guan Ping broke out and reached Linju (present-day Yuan'an, Hubei), where they were captured by Ma Zhong, a subordinate of Pan Zhang, and executed. Sun Quan sent Guan Yu's head to Cao Cao at Luoyang; Cao Cao buried the head with noble rites, while Sun Quan buried his body at Dangyang.

In this chapter Jingzhou passed to Eastern Wu, and the Sun–Liu alliance broke formally. The Fancheng offensive collapsed with it; Liu Bei lost the vital Jingzhou base, and the balance among the three powers was rewritten.
