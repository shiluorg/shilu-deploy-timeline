Russian Revolution of 1905 and the October Manifesto

On January 9 (December 22 Old Style, 'Bloody Sunday'), about 200,000 St.1,2 Petersburg workers marched to the Winter Palace to present a petition to the Tsar, but were shot at by troops, leaving about 4,600 dead and wounded. The revolution quickly spread nationwide: workers' strikes, peasant uprisings, and soldiers' mutinies (e.g., the Potemkin uprising). In October, a general strike broke out across the empire. Tsar Nicholas II was forced to issue the October Manifesto on October 17, promising to convene a State Duma (parliament) and guarantee civil liberties. Although the revolution was eventually suppressed, it forced the Tsar to implement constitutional reforms.1

  2. 2EventSourcedL2

    On January 22, 1905 (January 9, Old Style), troops fired on a peaceful demonstration in St. Petersburg, an event known as Bloody Sunday; at the end of October a general strike forced Nicholas II to issue the October Manifesto on October 30 (October 17, Old Style), promising an elected Duma and civil liberties.
