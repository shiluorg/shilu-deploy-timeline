Cao Pi usurps the Han throne and establishes the Wei dynasty; Eastern Han ends, Three Kingdoms tripartite equilibrium forms

Hanzhong: How the Triangle Solidified into Tripartite Rule
Chapter 7 of 7
Previous chapterCao Cao dies at Luoyang

Cao Cao's son Cao Pi, using political maneuvers, forced Emperor Xian of Han, Liu Xie, to abdicate and ascended the throne at Luoyang, proclaiming his dynasty Wei and changing the era name to Huangchu. The Eastern Han, restored by Emperor Guangwu, came to an end after 195 years. Cao Pi granted Liu Xie the title of Duke of Shanyang, with a fief of 10,000 households and generous treatment.

The following year Liu Bei proclaimed himself emperor at Chengdu, naming his dynasty Han (known to history as Shu Han) and claiming to continue the Han line. In 221 Sun Quan accepted Wei's enfeoffment as King of Wu, and in 229 he officially declared himself emperor. The Three Kingdoms tripartite equilibrium was fully established.

In the end, decades of warlord infighting closed into three coexisting regimes. The contests over Hanzhong, Jingzhou, and Fancheng in the preceding chapters form the geographic and military background of this settlement; the unification drive was fixed into three boundaries, and a new phase of confrontation began.
