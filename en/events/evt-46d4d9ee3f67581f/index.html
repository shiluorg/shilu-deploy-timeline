On March 15, 44 BCE, Caesar was killed by a group of senators in the Senate House within Pompey's theatre complex. The plot was led by Cassius and Brutus, and later accounts say some 60 men took part.

Caesar suffered an estimated 23 wounds; a physician reportedly claimed only one was fatal, but these figures come from later narratives, not an autopsy.

Caesar conquered Gaul in 58–50 BCE, won the civil war of 49–45 and held the dictatorship in 46–44. His concentration of power stripped the Senate of its normal role as arbiter and convinced some senators that only assassination could stop a return of kingship.

The assassination did not restore the Republic. After Caesar's death, Antony, Lepidus and Octavian formed the Second Triumvirate and civil war continued; the republican shell remained, but control of armies now decided power.
