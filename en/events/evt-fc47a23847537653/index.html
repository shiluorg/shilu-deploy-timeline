The First Punic War ran from 264 to 241 BCE and was the first great war between the Roman Republic and Carthage for control of Corsica and Sicily. In 264 Carthage intervened in a dispute between Messana and Syracuse and established a presence on the island; Rome then attacked Messana and forced the Carthaginians to withdraw.

The war was not quick. In 260 a Roman fleet failed to gain complete control of Sicily but opened the road to Corsica; in 256 a second fleet landed in Africa, but in 255 a new Carthaginian army built around cavalry and elephants drove the invaders back to the sea.

The fighting in Sicily resumed in 254 and remained largely stalemated until 241, when a fleet of 200 warships gave Rome control of the sea-lanes and the Carthaginian stronghold in Sicily collapsed. Carthage surrendered the next year, ceding Sicily and the Lipari Islands and agreeing to pay an indemnity.

Rome now had to maintain a fleet, overseas garrisons and a tax machine over the long term; the republic of 'temporary levies' began to be remade by war.
