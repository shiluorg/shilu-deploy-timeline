Battle of Salamis

In 480 BCE the Athenian statesman Themistocles lured the large Persian fleet into the narrow strait of Salamis. The Greek allied fleet had about 380 ships; the Persians had about 1,000.

The Greeks used the confined water to prevent the Persian fleet from deploying, and their more manoeuvrable triremes won a decisive victory. The Persians lost about 200 ships, the Greeks only 40. Xerxes I was forced to withdraw to Asia, and the war turned in Greece's favour.

Salamis showed how sea power and geography could turn numbers into a burden. The Persian fleet was vast on open water but lost its formation in a narrow channel; the Athenian fleet made the channel its own battlefield. The naval victory did not end the war at once, but it removed the Persian ability to outflank Greece by sea, and the next decisive contest moved to land.
