Sargon of Akkad unifies Mesopotamia

Around 2334 BCE Sargon of Akkad, first a courtier of the king of Kish, staged a coup and founded the Akkadian Empire. He defeated Lugalzagesi, conquered the Sumerian city-states and campaigned in Elam, Syria and Anatolia.

The event record says Sargon created the first standing army and unified administrative divisions, called himself “king of the four quarters”, and was the first ruler to build a multi-ethnic empire. These “firsts” are the record's claims; this chapter repeats them without enlarging them.

Sargon's significance lies less in being more ruthless than a city king than in placing many city-states inside a higher military and administrative framework. The independent competition of the city-states was suppressed by imperial command; Sumerian urban civilisation did not end with city-state politics, but it now had to find a place inside an empire.
