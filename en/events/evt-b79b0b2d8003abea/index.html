Saadian Morocco defeats Songhai near Timbuktu, shifting West Sudan hegemony.

In 1591 a Moroccan army with firearms defeated Songhai, showing a changing balance of power across the Sahara. Songhai collapsed, but Timbuktu’s scholarly and commercial traditions did not vanish overnight.

In 1591 a Saadian army crossed the Sahara, defeated the Songhai main force near Tondibi and occupied Timbuktu and Gao.1 The Songhai Empire collapsed and the western Sudan's gold trade shifted north.2 The Moroccans could not control the countryside for long, and local kingdoms and clans reasserted themselves.3 The expedition showed that trans-Saharan power depended on firearms, supply and caravan nodes; once those broke, an empire could shrink quickly.4
