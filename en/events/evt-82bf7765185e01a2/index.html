The Oceanic Turn: From Cape of Good Hope to Circumnavigation
Chapter 1 of 6
Next chapterColumbus discovers the Americas

Dias sailed south along West Africa with two caravels and, near thirty degrees south, was driven off the coast by a storm for some two weeks; turning east and finding no land, he realised he had passed the tip of the continent and ran north to what is now Mossel Bay. He sighted the headland on the return and named it the Cape of Storms, which King John II renamed the Cape of Good Hope. The voyage proved the Atlantic and Indian oceans connected, opening the way for Vasco da Gama.

Rounding the Cape solved how to enter the Indian Ocean from the Atlantic; it did not solve whom to trade with once there. Opening a route and building commerce are two different things.
