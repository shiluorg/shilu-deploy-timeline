The Ebers Papyrus was compiled about 1550 BCE and is one of the oldest known medical works. It copied symptoms, prescriptions and spells into one scroll rather than sorting them into a modern manual.

It contains about 700 magical formulas and folk remedies, ranging from crocodile bite to toenail pain and including ways to rid a house of flies, rats and scorpions.

The scroll gives a relatively accurate description of the circulatory system: blood vessels run throughout the body, and the heart is the centre of the blood supply.

The papyrus was acquired in 1873 by the German Egyptologist Georg Ebers.

It placed experience, spell and drug within one practice; it was neither a modern clinical manual nor merely 'superstition'.
