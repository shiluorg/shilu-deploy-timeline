Bulguksa and Seokguram: faith carved in granite

In the 8th century the Silla prime minister Kim Dae-seong initiated and supervised the construction of Bulguksa and Seokguram; Bulguksa was built in 774. Bulguksa stands on the slopes of Mount Toham and consists of wooden halls on raised stone terraces, stone bridges and two pagodas, its layout intended to represent the land of Buddha.

Seokguram is an artificial grotto of granite with an antechamber, a corridor and a main rotunda, enshrining a seated Sakyamuni Buddha. Its dome and entrance passage use more than 360 stone slabs; the reliefs of bodhisattvas, disciples and guardian deities around the Buddha are regarded as a masterpiece of East Asian Buddhist art.

Stone and wood have different jobs here: wooden halls can be repaired, while terraces, bridges and pagodas fix the skeleton of the sacred space to the hillside; the grotto seals sculpture, worship route and engineering calculation inside one circular chamber. Unified Silla concentrated state wealth, Buddhist faith and engineering skill around Gyeongju, leaving a set of spaces one can still walk into.
