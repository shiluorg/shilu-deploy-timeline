Möngke elected Great Khan

In 1251 Batu convened a kurultai by the Altai River and raised Möngke, eldest son of Tolui, as the fourth great khan of the Mongol Empire. The princes of the Ögedei and Chagatai lines refused to recognize him, but Batu's military backing allowed Möngke to take the throne anyway.

Möngke then launched a purge, executing dozens of princes and commanders from the Ögedei and Chagatai lines. The succession was not a formal election but a power settlement imposed by armies and reprisals.

This accession made the empire's succession conflict public: the princely lines were no longer merely obeying; at the next kurultai they could refuse, or raise another khan. The seeds of the dual khanate and civil war between Kublai and Ariq Böke were planted here.

Original entry and sentence-level review

Batu convened a kurultai on the banks of the Alataw River, which elected Möngke, the eldest son of Tolui, as the fourth Great Khan of the Mongol Empire.1 Princes of the Ögedei and Chagatai lineages refused to recognize him, but Möngke, backed by Batu's strong military support, forcibly assumed the throne.2 He then launched a great purge, executing dozens of princes and generals from the Ögedei and Chagatai lines, consolidating his power.3
