Mongols destroy Dali kingdom

In 1253 Möngke sent his brother Kublai against the Dali kingdom. Instead of pushing along the Southern Song front, Kublai marched from Lintao in Gansu through Sichuan and eastern Tibet, covering more than three thousand li in a flanking march to Dali (present-day Dali, Yunnan).

The last ruler of Dali, Duan Xingzhi, was captured, and a kingdom that had lasted more than three hundred years (937–1253) ended. The event record says Yunnan entered direct central administration for the first time.

For the Mongols this was not a raid on a borderland but the opening of a southwestern flank against the Southern Song. Dali's mountains, population and roads toward Southeast Asia entered the empire's military map; for the local polity, it was the beginning of absorption into a larger empire.

Original entry and sentence-level review

Mongke Khan sent his brother Kublai to conquer the Dali Kingdom in present-day Yunnan.1 Kublai advanced in three columns from Lintao in Gansu, through Sichuan and eastern Tibet, and took the city of Dali in 1253.2 The last king, Duan Xingzhi, fled to Shanchan and was later captured; the Dali Kingdom, which had ruled Yunnan for more than three centuries, came to an end.3 Yunnan came under direct Mongol control, but the Duan family continued to govern locally as hereditary overseers.4
