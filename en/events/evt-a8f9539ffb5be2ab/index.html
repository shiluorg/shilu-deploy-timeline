In 490 BCE the Persian king Darius I sent Datis and Artaphernes with an army of about 25,000, which landed on the plain of Marathon. The Athenian general Miltiades led about 10,000 hoplites and 1,000 Plataean allies against them.

Using the terrain and a double-envelopment tactic, the Athenians defeated a larger force, killing about 6,400 while losing only 192 of their own. For an army of citizen soldiers, the exchange was extraordinary, and it greatly raised Greek confidence in resisting Persia.

Marathon was not an isolated encounter. It showed that hoplites, in the right terrain and with the right tactics, could beat a larger Persian army. But it did not end the Persian threat. Ten years later Xerxes I returned with a much larger force, and the Greek poleis had to face both land and sea.
