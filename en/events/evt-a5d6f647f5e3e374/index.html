First Ancient Olympic Games Held at Olympia

In 776 BCE the Greek poleis held the first Olympic Games at Olympia in the Peloponnese. The only event at first was a short sprint of about 192 metres, the stadion. The winner was Coroebus, a cook from Elis.

During the Games the poleis agreed a “sacred truce”: wars stopped so that athletes and spectators could travel safely. For a Greek world made of hundreds of poleis, this was more than a sporting contest. It was a shared time and a shared identity, a place where states could meet without first meeting on a battlefield.

The Olympic festival became the most important pan-Hellenic gathering and lasted more than a thousand years; it also inspired the modern Olympic movement. It proved that the poleis could form a community, but that community was religious and festive, not political. When the Games ended, the poleis returned to their borders and rivalries.
