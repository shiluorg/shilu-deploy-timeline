The shape of caste: four varnas and social stratification

Event
900 BCEAncient IndiaCultureLegacy import · pending source reviewL2 · Source linked
Story context
Indus and Vedic: Cities, Trade and the Origins of Caste
Chapter 8 of 8
Previous chapterLate Vedic: varna order and kingship

The four varnas gradually fixed in late Vedic society: Brahmans, Kshatriyas, Vaishyas and Shudras. The Brahman position rested on “ritual purity” and the qualification to recite the sacred texts; the warrior class held political power but needed Brahmans to supply legitimacy.

This stratification was not a legal table written in a day. Later caste society contained many local sub-castes and occupational groups, and the four varnas of the late Vedic period cannot be equated directly with caste as it exists today.

“The shape of caste” is more accurate than “the caste system was complete”. The framework of the four varnas had appeared, but local practice, occupational movement and marriage rules would keep evolving. The Indus cities left streets and drains; the Vedic tradition left identities and narratives. There is no smooth line of inheritance between them, yet together they form a deep layer of South Asian civilisation.

Sources: 3 claims (2 sourced); Britannica “Brahman (social class)”.
