Ahmose and the Hyksos: the military-fiscal New Kingdom

Ahmose I reigned c. 1539–1514 BCE, founded the 18th dynasty and completed the expulsion of the Hyksos.

He captured the Hyksos capital of Avaris, pursued the enemy to Sharuhen in Palestine, and took it after a three-year siege.

Before advancing into Palestine, Ahmose campaigned three times in Nubia, whose ruler had been an ally of the Hyksos.

After his borders were secure, he established an administration loyal to him, granted land to veterans and members of the royal family, and reopened the copper mines of Sinai and trade with the Syrian coast.

The New Kingdom was not a sudden revival but a military-fiscal machine joining the expulsion of foreigners, frontier warfare, land grants and temple rebuilding.
