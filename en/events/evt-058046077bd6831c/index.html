The Mongol Empire: From the Onon to the Volga
Chapter 1 of 12
Next chapterStriking Western Xia first, opening the southern flank

In 1206 the Mongol tribes held a kurultai at the source of the Onon River. Temujin was proclaimed Genghis Khan, "the khan who possesses the four quarters of the ocean". This was not an ordinary tribal election: it placed formerly warring tribes under one supreme khan.

The new polity did three things at once. It issued the Great Yassa, putting law into an executable text; it divided the whole Mongol population into ninety-five thousands, each granted to a founding commander on a hereditary basis; and it took ten thousand sons of noble families into the keshig, a guard that also trained senior commanders. A thousand was both a productive unit and a mobilization unit, so scattered steppe households could be assembled into armies.

For later western campaigns, the value of this system lay in its reproducibility: conquered regions could be organized into thousands, old and surrendered troops could be broken up and reassigned, and the guard supplied a cross-tribal command cadre. 1206 was therefore a beginning, not an end.

At a kurultai convened at the source of the Onon River, the Mongol tribes elected Temüjin as 'Genghis Khan', meaning 'Khan of the Oceans' or 'Universal Ruler'.1 He promulgated the 'Great Yassa' (Mongol code of laws) and established a strict military-civilian 'thousand-unit' system: the entire Mongol population was divided into 95 thousand-units, each under a hereditary commander from among his founding generals.2 He also created the kheshig (imperial guard), selecting 10,000 sons of nobles to form an elite guard that both protected the khan and served as a training ground for senior commanders.3 The establishment of the Mongol Empire fundamentally altered the subsequent course of Eurasian history.4,5
