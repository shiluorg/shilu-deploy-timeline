Mongol invasion of Europe begins

Story context
The Mongol Empire: From the Onon to the Volga
Chapter 9 of 12
Previous chapterThe deathbed orders below Liupan MountainNext chapterKiev: the disappearance of a capital

In 1235 Ögedei ordered a second western campaign. Unlike the first, it was led by the eldest sons of the imperial lines, and so became known as the "princes' campaign". Batu, eldest son of Jochi, was commander; Subutai was deputy; also present were Güyük, eldest son of Ögedei, Möngke, eldest son of Tolui, and Büri, eldest grandson of Chagatai.

The event record states that some hundred and twenty thousand troops set out from the Mongolian plateau to conquer the Kipchak steppe and the Rus' principalities. Each imperial line sent its eldest son, which widened the manpower base and gave every branch of the dynasty a stake in the war.

The campaign eventually produced the Golden Horde. It pushed the empire's northwestern frontier to the Volga and into the Rus' heartland; but the "eldest sons" arrangement also brought the tensions of succession politics into the army, and later withdrawal and the division of khanates were bound up with it.

Original entry and sentence-level review

Great Khan Ögedei decided to launch a second western campaign, with armies commanded by the eldest sons of the imperial clan, hence called the 'Princes' Western Campaign'.1 The supreme commander was Batu, son of Jochi; his deputy was Subutai.2 Other participants included Güyük (son of Ögedei), Möngke (son of Tolui), and Büri (grandson of Chagatai).3 The army of about 120,000 set out from the Mongolian plateau to conquer the Kipchak steppes and the Rus' principalities, ultimately leading to the establishment of the Golden Horde.4
