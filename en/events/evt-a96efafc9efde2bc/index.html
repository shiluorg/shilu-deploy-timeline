Orhan takes Bursa and gives the Ottomans a capital

Chapter 2 of 6

On April 6, 1326, Orhan took Bursa; other sources put the fall in 1324. Osman himself did not see that day. Tradition says he died around 1324, and another account says Bursa fell shortly after his death.

Orhan made Bursa his capital and turned a Byzantine city into the Ottoman court, tax centre and religious centre. Conquest was no longer only about dividing plunder; it required collecting taxes, minting coins, paying troops and building schools.

In 1327 Orhan minted the first Ottoman silver coins, stamped “Orhan, son of Osman.” In the same period he began turning the ghazi raiders into paid regular infantry and cavalry.

Bursa's mosques, colleges and caravansaries plugged the new state into the Islamic urban network. Caravansaries connected Anatolian and Rumelian trade, colleges trained judges and officials, and mosques gave the ruler public legitimacy.

That is what a capital does: it converts war revenue into repeatable institutions. Without Bursa, the Ottomans remained an armed frontier group; with Bursa, they began to look like a state.

Original entry and sentence-level review

On April 6, 1326, Orhan captured the Byzantine city of Bursa; some sources date the fall to 1324. Ottoman tradition holds that Osman died shortly after the city fell, while some scholars place his death in 1324.1

Bursa became Orhan's capital. It concentrated war revenue in a fixed city: court, taxation and religious schools took root there, and the Ottomans ceased to be only a mobile frontier force.2

In 1327 Orhan minted the first silver Ottoman coins in his own name and reorganized the army into paid regular infantry (yaya) and cavalry (müsellem), reducing dependence on nomadic raiders.3

Orhan also built mosques, theological colleges and caravansaries in Bursa and newly conquered towns. The city turned military revenue into durable institutions rather than capital for the next raid.4
