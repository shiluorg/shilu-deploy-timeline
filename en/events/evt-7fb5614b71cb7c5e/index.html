The Narmer Palette: unification drawn as one king

Tradition makes Menes the first king to unify Upper and Lower Egypt and credits him with founding the 1st dynasty. The name appears in later king lists and later histories, not in a contemporary coronation record.

Modern scholars have not conclusively identified Menes with Narmer, Aha or other early kings.

The Narmer Palette was found at Hierakonpolis, dates to the beginning of the 1st dynasty, c. 2900 BCE, and shows Narmer wearing the White Crown.

The other side is usually read as the Red Crown; the two crowns place Upper and Lower Egypt under one royal name, but this is commemorative imagery, not an administrative record.

Unification more likely took several generations: the real integration lay in tax rolls, borders, a capital and local officials, not in the aftermath of one battle.
