Oleg Seizes Kiev, 'the Mother of Rus' Cities'

This translation predates the current source revision and needs review. Chinese source ↗

When Rurik died in 879 he entrusted his realm and infant son Igor to his kinsman Oleg as regent.1 In 882 Oleg moved south from Novgorod along the Dnieper, taking Smolensk and Lyubech before reaching Kiev.2 According to the Primary Chronicle he disguised his boats as a merchant convoy, lured out the Varangian rulers Askold and Dir, presented Igor with the words 'here is the son of Rurik', and had them killed. Oleg made Kiev his capital, declaring it 'the mother of Rus' cities', joining northern Novgorod to southern Kiev and securing the whole trade route 'from the Varangians to the Greeks'. Kievan Rus' took shape from this moment.

    • OlegOpening paragraphs
      According to The Russian Primary Chronicle of the 12th century, Oleg, after succeeding his kinsman Rurik as ruler of Novgorod (c. 879), went down the Dnieper River with his Varangian retinue and seized control of Smolensk and Kiev (882), which he subsequently made his capital. Extending his authority east and west of the Volkhov–Dnieper waterway, he united the local Slavic and Finnish tribes under his rule and became the undisputed ruler of the Kievan–Novgorodian state.
