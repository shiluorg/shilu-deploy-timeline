Liu Bei ascends the imperial throne at Chengdu; his dynasty is called Han, known to history as Shu Han

Liu Bei: From a Wandering Leader to Emperor in Chengdu
Chapter 6 of 8
Previous chapterJingzhou dispute temporarily easedNext chapterCampaign against Wu

In the fourth month of 221, urged on by Zhuge Liang and his other ministers, Liu Bei raised an altar south of Mount Wudan at Chengdu and took the imperial title, keeping "Han" as the dynasty name; historians call it Shu Han. The event record says he had heard reports that Cao Pi had usurped the throne and that Emperor Xian had been killed; in fact Xian had merely been demoted to Duke of Shanyang and was still alive. Liu Bei held mourning for him, gave him the posthumous name Xiaomin, and presented himself as the rightful heir of the Han.

He proclaimed the Zhangwu era and appointed Zhuge Liang chancellor, Xu Jing minister over the masses, Zhang Fei General of Chariots and Cavalry, and Ma Chao General of Agile Cavalry. The record states the three-way division between Wei, Shu, and Wu was now fixed.

In the person-centered line, this chapter forms the Liu Bei regime: after borrowing land and entering Shu, he founded his state under the Han name and took the throne. The record does not detail the accession rites or the recognition extended by other powers.
