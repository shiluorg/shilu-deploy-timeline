The Fourth Crusade contracts with Venice for transport

Original entry and sentence-level review

Pope Innocent III had called for a new crusade in August 1198 with the bull Post miserabile, but Europe's monarchs were preoccupied and the response was thin.1,2,3,4,5 In 1199 Count Theobald of Champagne took the cross at a tournament at Écry, and knights of northern France and Flanders followed.6 In April 1201 the crusade's envoys signed a transport treaty at Venice with the aged and nearly blind Doge Enrico Dandolo: Venice would provide a fleet for 33,500 men and 4,500 horses with a year's provisions, for 85,000 marks of silver and half of all conquests.7 Theobald died that year and Boniface of Montferrat was chosen commander in his place.8 Fewer than a third of the expected troops assembled and they could not pay; Venice therefore pressed them first against the Christian city of Zara and then on Constantinople, sacked in 1204—an outcome utterly remote from the recovery of Jerusalem.9
