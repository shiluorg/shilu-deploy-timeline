Assyria Captures Samaria; Kingdom of Israel Falls

Israel and Judah: Temple, Schism and Exile

Previous chapterKing Solomon dies, the Kingdom of Israel splits

The Assyrian army finally captured Samaria. The Assyrian king deported the inhabitants of Israel to various parts of the Assyrian Empire, creating the 'Lost Ten Tribes'. Israelite territory was turned into an Assyrian province, and foreign immigrants were settled there, forming the later Samaritans. The kingdom of Israel was completely destroyed.

Assyria captured Samaria and the northern kingdom fell. Much of the population was deported and remaining inhabitants mixed with incoming groups; Judah interpreted the disaster as the consequence of apostasy and wrote it into later texts.

After Samaria fell, Assyria deported large numbers of Israelites and settled immigrants from other parts of the empire.2 'The lost ten tribes' is a later name for these assimilated populations, not a single complete migration.1 The Samaria region developed a mixed religious community and drifted apart from the southern kingdom of Judah.3 Assyrian deportation aimed to break local resistance networks, but it also created new ethnic boundaries.
