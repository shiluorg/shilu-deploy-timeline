Zhuge Liang's first northern expedition: Ma Su loses Jieting, leading to complete failure

Zhuge Liang: From the Longzhong Plan to the Wu Zhang Plains
Chapter 7 of 8
Previous chapterPresents Chu Shi Biao, advances to HanzhongNext chapterDies of illness at Wu Zhang Plains

In 228, Zhuge Liang feinted an attack via the Xie Valley route but actually led his main force out of Mount Qi, modern Li County, Gansu. The event record says Nan'an, Tianshui, and Anding commanderies rebelled and responded to him, throwing the Guanzhong region into turmoil.

Emperor Ming of Wei personally traveled to Chang'an to take command and sent Zhang He to the rescue. Zhuge Liang ordered Ma Su to hold Jieting, modern Zhuanglang, Gansu. Ma Su disobeyed orders by camping on a hill; Zhang He cut off his water supply, and the Shu army broke up in disorder. Zhuge Liang executed Ma Su and withdrew all his forces to Hanzhong; the first northern expedition failed in its closest approach.

In the person-centered line, this was the expedition closest to its goal: the response of three commanderies was lost through the loss at Jieting. The record does not detail the local terrain of the Jieting battle or the schedule of the withdrawal.
