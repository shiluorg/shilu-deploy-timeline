Sun Quan accepts Wei titles but refuses to send a hostage; proclaims himself King of Wu and adopts reign name Huangwu

Sun Quan: A Successor's Vassalage and Imperial Claim
Chapter 5 of 8
Previous chapterReceives Wei's title King of WuNext chapterProclaims emperor at Wuchang

After the defeat at Xiaoting, Sun Quan, while nominally submissive to Wei, refused to send his son Sun Deng as a hostage to Luoyang. The event record says Cao Pi demanded the hostage several times, was refused each time, and Wei–Wu relations deteriorated.

Sun Quan changed his era name to Huangwu, indicating he no longer used Wei's era name, and established his own bureaucracy, in effect becoming an independent state.

In the person-centered line, this chapter preserves Sun Quan's autonomy: vassalage was nominal, and he did not trade his crown prince for Wei's trust. Instead, he drew on an independent system through a new era name and his own officials. The record does not detail the official structure set up under Huangwu.

Sun Quan accepted Cao Pi's title to buy time, but refused to send his son Sun Deng to Luoyang as a hostage.1 When Wei demanded the hostage and was refused, relations deteriorated.2 By changing the era name to Huangwu and appointing his own officials, Sun Quan declared in ritual terms that he was not a Wei subject.3 Wu and Shu renewed their alliance, and Sun avoided a two-front war after Yiling.4 Taking the title of king rather than emperor was a balance: he needed Wei's recognition to manage Jiangdong elites, but did not want to claim the throne before he was strong enough.5
