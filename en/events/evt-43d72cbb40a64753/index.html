Construction of the Parthenon begins on the Acropolis

The Parthenon stands on the Acropolis, dedicated to Athena, the city's patron goddess; in the age of Pericles it also served as the treasury of the Delian League. Work began in 447 BCE, the main structure was complete in 438, and decoration continued until 432.

Ictinus and Callicrates designed the building, while Phidias oversaw the project and its sculptural decoration. Doric columns, pediments, frieze and statues turned myth, cult and civic identity into one visual order.

Set back into the finances of the Delian League, the Parthenon was not only a temple: allied tribute flowed into Athens and was converted into a monument on the Acropolis. It became the best-known building of classical Athens and a lasting reference for Western architecture; but that “classical” image also carries the shadow of imperial power.

Original entry and sentence-level review

The Parthenon stands on the Athenian Acropolis, is dedicated to the goddess Athena, and also served as the treasury of the Delian League.1 Construction began in 447 BC, the building was substantially complete in 438 BC, and decorative work continued until 432 BC.2 It was designed by Ictinus and Callicrates, with Phidias supervising the overall project and the sculptural decoration.3
