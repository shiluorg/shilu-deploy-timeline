Copernicus’s De revolutionibus was published in Nuremberg by Johannes Petreius in 1543.1 The first-edition title page held by the Library of Congress records the author, place of publication, printer and year.2 Placing “Publication of Copernicus’s De revolutionibus” in 1543 Europe, it connects earlier and later change in Technology.3 For readers, the significance of “Publication of Copernicus’s De revolutionibus” is best read through its connections with the events around it.4

In 1543, Copernicus's Theory of the Revolutions of the Heavenly Spheres was published in Nuremberg by Johannes Petreius.5 The title page of the first edition held by the Library of Congress records the author, place of publication, printer, and year of publication.5 Putting "the publication of Copernicus' "On the Revolutions of the Celestial Spheres" back to Poland in 1543, it connects the changes in technology over a long period of time.6 For readers, the significance of "the publication of Copernicus's "On the Revolutions of the Heavenly Spheres" must be understood from its connection with the events before and after it.
