Genghis Khan's western campaign against Khwarazm

In 1217 Genghis Khan launched the first large-scale western campaign, citing the Khwarazmian massacre of a Mongol caravan and the insult to Mongol envoys. The event record states that about a hundred and fifty thousand troops advanced in four columns, using converging attacks and taking Otrar, Bukhara and other major towns.

The Khwarazmian shah, Muhammad, did not concentrate his main army for a decisive battle under one city. Fearing Mongol mobility and field strength, he gave up resistance, withdrew toward Persia and finally fled to an island in the Caspian, where he died.

The campaign pushed the empire's frontier into Central Asia and Persia. For the Mongols it was the first transfer of war from the steppe and North China into settled agricultural regions and international trading cities; for Khwarazm, the caravan bloodshed brought not a border reprisal but a process of state destruction.

Original entry and sentence-level review

Genghis Khan launched his first major western campaign against the Khwarazmian Empire, using as pretext the massacre of a Mongol trade caravan and the humiliation of Mongol envoys.1 Leading about 150,000 troops divided into four columns, he employed a strategy of converging attacks, quickly capturing the major cities of Otrar, Bukhara, and others.2 The Khwarazmshah Muhammad, fearful of the Mongols' strength, abandoned resistance and fled to an island in the Caspian Sea, where he died.3 This campaign expanded the Mongol Empire into Central Asia and Persia.4,5
