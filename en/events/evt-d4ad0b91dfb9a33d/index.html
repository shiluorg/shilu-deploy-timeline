Original entry and sentence-level review

Around 800 BC, Phoenician colonists from Tyre founded Carthage (near present-day Tunis) in North Africa.1,2 Carthage exploited its advantageous geographic position to control western Mediterranean trade, rapidly developing into a maritime empire. Carthaginians conducted a circumnavigation of Africa (c. 600 BC) and established trading posts in West Africa, exchanging gold, ivory, and slaves with local tribes. Carthage was eventually destroyed by Rome in the Punic Wars (264-146 BC), and its African territories became the Roman province of Africa.1
