Pu Hong, chieftain of the Di tribe, leads his people to migrate to Guanzhong

350 CE

Pu Jian establishes the Former Qin regime, making Chang'an its capital

351 CE

Former Qin's Pu Jian formally proclaims himself emperor

355 CE

Former Qin founding emperor Pu Jian dies; the tyrant Fu Sheng succeeds

357 CE

Fu Jian kills Fu Sheng, proclaims himself emperor of Former Qin, with Wang Meng as chief minister

357 CE

Wang Meng is appointed Palace Attendant and Head of the Secretariat in Former Qin, promoting legal reforms

358 CE

Wang Meng directs civil service reform in Former Qin, dismissing hundreds of incompetent officials

367 CE

Fu Shuang and others, brothers of Former Qin's Fu Jian, launch a rebellion

368 CE

Wang Meng's reforms are fully implemented; legal construction in Former Qin bears fruit

369 CE

Former Yan general Murong Chui is suspected and forced to defect to Former Qin

370 CE

Former Qin destroys Former Yan, unifying northern China

372 CE

Former Qin's Fu Jian strengthens control over the Western Regions, opening the Silk Road

375 CE

Former Qin Grand Chancellor Wang Meng dies; Fu Jian loses his pillar

376 CE

Former Qin destroys Former Liang, unifying the last region of the north

377 CE

Fu Jian of Former Qin actively prepares to attack the Eastern Jin

378 CE

Former Qin captures the Eastern Jin stronghold Xiangyang, opening the gateway to the south

379 CE

Former Qin continues its southern advance; the Eastern Jin situation becomes critical

381 CE

Fu Jian, ignoring opposition, fully prepares for a southern campaign against the Eastern Jin

382 CE

The Former Qin army is fully assembled; Fu Jian vows to march south
