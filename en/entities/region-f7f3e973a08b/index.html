OPEN HISTORY

Western Jin

NameNames
An imported name and related records, not a verified political lifespan or modern territorial claim.
265 CE

Sima Yan enfeoffs twenty‑seven imperial clan members as kings, allowing them to maintain armies, planting the seeds of the War of the Eight Princes

265 CE

Sima Zhao dies; his son Sima Yan succeeds as King of Jin, then forces the Wei emperor to abdicate, establishing the Western Jin

266 CE

Emperor Wu of Jin reorganizes internal administration, implements the household tax system, and cuts luxury expenses

267 CE

Western Jin cartographer Pei Xiu draws the 'Yu Gong Regional Map' and proposes the six principles of cartography

267 CE

Small‑scale skirmishes break out between Western Jin and Eastern Wu on the Jingzhou border

269 CE

The Xianbei Tufa tribe rebels in Liangzhou; Jin forces are sent but suffer defeat

270 CE

The war between Western Jin and the Xianbei Tufa Shujineng continues, with heavy Jin losses

271 CE

Liu Shan, Later Lord of Shu Han, dies in Luoyang; posthumous title Duke Si

271 CE

Jin general Yang Hu pursues a policy of conciliation in Jingzhou, maintaining peace on the border with Wu's Lu Kang

272 CE

Wang Jun, governor of Yizhou under Western Jin, begins building large warships in Shu, preparing to attack Wu

273 CE

Emperor Wu of Jin, seeking to fill his harem, orders a selection of beauties and forbids marriage throughout the realm

274 CE

Yang Hu presents his memorial 'On the Necessity of Attacking Wu', urging Emperor Wu of Jin to attack immediately

275 CE

The Xianbei Tufa Shujineng continues his rebellion, capturing the major city of Liangzhou

275 CE

Emperor Wu of Jin changes the era name to Xianning, symbolizing an era of peace

276 CE

Yang Hu, gravely ill, on his deathbed recommends Du Yu to succeed him and continue planning the conquest of Wu

277 CE

Western Jin builds large numbers of warships in Yizhou and Jingzhou, training its navy to destroy Wu

278 CE

Du Yu carries out night raids in Jingzhou, capturing outposts on the Wu side of Jiangling

279 CE

Ma Long leads 3,500 men west, puts down the Xianbei rebellion in Liangzhou

279 CE

Wang Jun, governor of Yizhou of Western Jin, completes construction of a huge fleet in Shu, ready to sail east to conquer Wu

279 CE

Western Jin launches a six‑pronged invasion of Wu, with over 200,000 men, by land and water, beginning the campaign to destroy Wu

280 CE

Western Jin advances by land and water, captures Jianye, and ends Eastern Wu; the Three Kingdoms are finally unified

280 CE

After the conquest of Wu, Western Jin orders the reduction of provincial armies, simplifying the military establishment

280 CE

After the unification of China, Western Jin enjoys the Taikang era of peace, with social and economic recovery

281 CE

The Jijun tomb in Western Jin yields a large cache of Warring States bamboo slips, including the 'Bamboo Annals'

281 CE

Sun Hao, the Last Emperor of Wu, dies in Luoyang; his title is Marquis of Guiming

282 CE

The Taikang prosperity continues to develop under Western Jin, with a growing economy and population

283 CE

Emperor Wu of Jin orders the princes to go to their fiefs, but many remain in the capital

284 CE

The Xianbei Murong Gui sends envoys to pay tribute to Western Jin and is appointed Xianbei governor

284 CE

Western Jin continues its policy of rest and development, with a marked increase in the number of households

286 CE

Western Jin aristocrats Shi Chong and Wang Kai compete in ostentatious luxury

287 CE

Under Western Jin, official extravagance and bribery become rampant; government becomes corrupt

288 CE

Emperor Wu of Jin indulges in pleasure, and government affairs decline

289 CE

After the unification, Western Jin enjoys economic and cultural prosperity; Taikang literature flourishes

289 CE

Emperor Wu of Jin enfeoffs the imperial princes as regional commanders with real military power, planting the seeds of civil war

290 CE

Jia Nanfeng is installed as empress; later she will meddle in politics

290 CE

The consort clan member Yang Jun monopolizes the government, excluding the imperial princes

290 CE

Emperor Wu of Jin (Sima Yan) dies of illness, ending the Taikang prosperity

291 CE

Zhang Hua becomes Minister of Works, maintaining relative stability under Empress Jia

291 CE

First phase of the War of the Eight Princes: Empress Jia unites with Sima Liang and Sima Wei to eliminate Yang Jun, then turns on them

291 CE

Empress Jia kills Sima Liang and Wei Guan, seizing full control of the government

292 CE

Empress Jia places her own people in key positions to consolidate power, but state affairs remain under Zhang Hua

294 CE

The Xiongnu rebel Hao San rises in Shangdang, but is soon defeated

295 CE

A great fire breaks out in the Luoyang arsenal, burning a huge quantity of weapons and armor

296 CE

Drought in Guanzhong leads to a large influx of refugees into Shu, causing social unrest

296 CE

The Di tribal leader Qi Wannian rises in Guanzhong and proclaims himself emperor

297 CE

The Jin general Zhou Chu fights at the Battle of Liuwu without support and dies in combat

297 CE

Suppressing Qi Wannian's rebellion greatly increases Western Jin's military expenditure, depleting national strength

298 CE

Jin general Meng Guan leads elite troops to suppress Qi Wannian's rebellion, killing Qi Wannian

c. 298 CE

Famine continues in Guanzhong; Li Te leads refugees into Shu to seek food

299 CE

Empress Jia deposes the crown prince Sima Yu, provoking widespread anger

299 CE

Social contradictions intensify in Western Jin; refugee uprisings erupt one after another

301 CE

Prince of Zhao Sima Lun usurps the throne and proclaims himself emperor

302 CE

Prince of Changsha Sima Yi kills Prince of Qi Sima Jiong

303 CE

Zhang Chang rebels in Jiangxia

304 CE

Prince of Donghai Sima Yue imprisons Prince of Changsha Sima Yi

305 CE

Prince of Donghai Sima Yue rises against Prince of Hejian Sima Yong and Prince of Chengdu Sima Ying

306 CE

Emperor Hui of Jin is poisoned to death; Emperor Huai of Jin ascends the throne

307 CE

Wang Dao devises the strategy of 'govern with tranquility and attract all talents' for Sima Rui

307 CE

Sima Rui is stationed at Jianye, beginning to manage Jiangdong

313 CE

Zu Ti leads his troops north on a punitive expedition, recovering vast lands south of the Yellow River

313 CE

Emperor Min of Jin Sima Ye is enthroned in Chang'an

317 CE

Liu Kun fights alone in Bingzhou, allying with Xianbei against Han-Zhao
