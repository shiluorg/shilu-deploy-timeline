Fall of Constantinople (1453)

SourceSources
Fall of Constantinople (1453) · Encyclopaedia Britannica (accessed 2026-11-25) Original source ↗

选段

The Fall of Constantinople occurred on May 29, 1453, when the city was conquered by Sultan Mehmed II of the Ottoman Empire. The Ottomans breached Constantinople's ancient land wall after a 55-day siege, ending the dwindling Byzantine Empire.

Constantinople's population had dropped from roughly 400,000 in the 12th century to between 40,000 and 50,000 by the 1450s as a result of constant battles for dominance with its Balkan neighbors and Roman Catholic rivals.

Between 60,000 and 80,000 soldiers fought on land, accompanied by 69 cannon.

On April 6 the Ottomans began their artillery barrage and brought down a section of the wall. They mounted a frontal assault of the land walls on April 7, but the Byzantines repelled them and were able to repair the defenses.

Mehmed ordered a third attack on the gate, this time with one of his own palace regiments of 3,000 Janissaries. A small group reached the top of a tower through another gate but were nearly eliminated by the defenders until Giustiniani was mortally wounded by Ottoman gunfire while on the ramparts.

Although the sultan attempted to prevent a total sack of the city, he permitted an initial period of looting that saw the destruction of many Orthodox churches. When most of Constantinople was secure, Mehmed himself rode through the streets of the city to the great cathedral of Hagia Sophia, the largest in all of Christendom, and converted it into the mosque Ayasofya.

The city was then pillaged for three days before Mehmed II restored order and made it his new capital.
