选段

Battle of Cannae, (August 216 BCE), battle fought near the ancient village of Cannae, in southern Apulia (modern Puglia), southeastern Italy, between the forces of Rome and Carthage during the Second Punic War. … recorded Roman losses ranging from 55,000 (according to Roman historian Livy) to 70,000 (according to Greek historian Polybius). … it is regarded by military historians as a classic example of a victorious double envelopment.

Hannibal was the first to arrive at the battle site, with a force of about 40,000 infantry and 10,000 cavalry. … Hannibal confined the eight Roman legions in a narrow valley, hemmed in by the river. … the Roman consuls Lucius Aemilius Paullus and Gaius Terentius Varro brought to Cannae roughly 80,000 men, about half of whom lacked significant battle experience.

He stationed his Gallic and Spanish infantry in the centre, two groups of his African troops on their flanks and the cavalry on the wings. … his line adopted a crescent shape … although it fell back, it did not break … Hannibal’s crescent became a circle, with Hannibal’s African and Spanish troops on the wings pressing inward on the Romans and the Carthaginian cavalry attacking from the rear.

Among the Roman dead were 28 of 40 tribunes, up to 80 Romans of Senatorial or high magistrate rank, and at least 200 knights (Romans of equestrian rank). It was estimated that 20 percent of Roman fighting men between the ages of 18 and 50 died at Cannae. Only 14,000 Roman soldiers escaped, and 10,000 more were captured; the rest were killed. The Carthaginians lost about 6,000 men.

Hannibal was exhorted to march on Rome by Maharbal, one of his commanders, but Hannibal did not do so. … Hannibal had hoped that many South Italians would desert Rome and ally with him after his crushing victory. … Rome resumed the Fabian strategy, denying Hannibal the opportunity to achieve a second victory of Cannae’s scale, and Hannibal saw the strength of his armies and his allies whittled away through slow attrition.
