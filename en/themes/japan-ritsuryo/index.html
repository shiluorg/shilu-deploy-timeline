Japan's Ritsuryo State: Buddhism, Prince Shotoku and Nara

Theme

In 552 King Seong of Baekje sent Buddhist scriptures, an image, and liturgical ornaments to the Japanese court; 538 is also cited. Prince Shōtoku became regent in 593 and later instituted the twelve court ranks; in 604 he issued the Seventeen-Article Constitution. After the 645 coup, the reform rescript declared land and people public; the Taihō code was compiled in 701; in 710 the capital moved to Heijō-kyō, in 741 the kokubunji system was established, and in 752 the Great Buddha of Tōdai-ji was dedicated; the Nara period lasted until 784.

ONE EDITORIAL QUESTION

Between 552 and 784, how did Japan turn Buddhism, legal codes and the capital into instruments of kingship, and how much clan politics remained?
